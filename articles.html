REF_ID: 102_INDEFINITE_SOUND

A vs. An: Sound Over Spelling

(•‿•)
Artie is loading...

Indefinite articles a and an do not refer to a particular noun. We choose between them based on the first sound of the following word, not its spelling:

  • Use A before singular countable nouns beginning with a consonant sound (e.g. a cot, a rug).
  • Use AN before singular countable nouns beginning with a vowel sound (e.g. an ostrich).

Exceptions and Silent Letters

Condition Rule Example Explanation
Vowel spelling, consonant sound a university / a European Pronounced with "yu" sound (consonant /j/)
Vowel spelling, "w" sound a one-sided story Pronounced like "wa" (consonant /w/)
Consonant spelling, vowel sound an MLA / an NCC cadet / an SOS Letters start with vowel sounds: "em", "en", "es"
Silent H an honest man / an hour / an heir "H" is silent, so the word starts with a vowel sound

Indefinite Articles: 12 Usage Contexts

(•‿•)

The textbook outlines 12 specific instances where a or an is used:

# Rule Context Book Example
1 Single countable noun used for first time A lady got down from a taxi.
2 In the sense of "one" She had a toast and an apple for breakfast.
3 Singular countable noun in general sense A judge ensures that justice is done.
4 To refer to "each" (rate/measure) The electrician charges ₹1,000 an hour.
5 Phrases expressing speed, quantity, price Tomatoes cost ₹50 a kilo.
6 Phrases indicating numbers She bought a couple of shirts. / a hundred flowers.
7 To imply the whole class in a general sense A cheetah can run very fast.
8 Proper noun used as a common noun She is a Serena Williams in the making.
9 Exclamatory phrases What a funny clown he is! / What an awesome gift!
10 Strangers with titles A Mr Sinha has come to meet you.
11 Occupations My father is an architect.
12 Certain verbal phrases Give me a break. / Earn a living.

REF_ID: 106_DEFINITE_THE

The Definite Article: The

(•‿•)
Artie is loading...

The definite article the points to someone or something in particular. It is used:

  • When referring to a noun already mentioned (e.g. I bought a car. The car has six airbags.).
  • Before a singular noun representing a whole class (e.g. The octopus has eight tentacles.).
  • Before adjectives used as nouns (e.g. The injured were given first aid.).
  • Before superlative adjectives (e.g. the best athlete, the tallest member).
  • With proper nouns of specific categories (rivers, mountain ranges, holy books, well-known buildings, etc.).

Definite Article with Proper Nouns

Category Verbatim Book Examples
Hotels, Cinemas, Theatres The Royal Hotel, The Globe Cinema
Rivers, Seas, Oceans, Deserts The Yamuna, The Red Sea, The Indian Ocean, The Sahara Desert
Mountain Ranges The Alps, The Western Ghats (Note: omit before single peaks like Mount Everest)
Holy / Famous Books The Guru Granth Sahib, The Sun Also Rises
Newspapers & Magazines The Indian Express, The Week
Ships & Trains The Rajdhani Express, The HMS Victory
Plural Country/Island Names The Maldives, The Philippines
Adjective-Noun Countries The United Kingdom, The United Arab Emirates

REF_ID: 108_OMISSION

Omission of Articles

(•‿•)
Artie is loading...

Knowing when NOT to use an article (no article / marked as x) is vital! We omit articles before:

  • Proper Nouns: e.g. Jaipur, Rajasthan, Amit.
  • Common Nouns in widest sense: e.g. Children love to play., Man is mortal.
  • Abstract Nouns: e.g. Laughter is infectious., Honesty is a virtue.
  • Material Nouns: e.g. Gold is precious., This bracelet is made of silver.
  • Languages: e.g. speak German. (Unless specifying: the French language).
  • Meals and Games: e.g. prepared lunch, Cricket is my favorite sport.
PRIMARY VS SECONDARY PURPOSE We omit articles before places like hospital, school, college, church, bed, temple, prison when referring to their primary purpose:
  • Primary: I go to school daily. (to study) / The patient is in bed. (to rest)
  • Secondary: My father went to the school to meet the principal. (not to study) / He sat on the bed. (as a piece of furniture)

REF_ID: 110_REPETITION

Repetition of Articles

(•‿•)
Artie is loading...

Repeating an article alters the count of subjects and grammatical agreement:

  • One item, single article: a pink and purple gown (one gown, two colors).
  • Two items, multiple articles: a pink and a purple gown (two separate gowns).
  • Same Person: The actor and director was awarded. (single person holding two roles, article before first noun only).
  • Different Persons: The actor and the director were awarded. (two separate individuals, articles before both nouns).
AGREEMENT NOTE Observe how the verb agrees: was awarded (singular) for the same person, versus were awarded (plural) for two different persons.

REF_ID: 112_QUANTIFIERS

Tricky Quantifiers: Few vs. Little

(•‿•)
Artie is loading...

The choice between few (countable) and little (uncountable) and their articled forms is a frequent ICSE board exam target:

Type Hardly Any (Negative meaning) A Small Number / Amount (Positive) All that is there (Specific/Definite)
Countable (e.g. students, books) Few
Hardly any students passed.		 A few
A few students passed (some).		 The few
The few students who passed were happy.
Uncountable (e.g. water, milk, hope) Little
There is little hope left.		 A little
A little hope is enough (some).		 The little
The little hope he had faded away.
