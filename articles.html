REF_ID: 102_INDEFINITE_SOUND

A vs. An: Sound Over Spelling

(•‿•)
Artie is loading...

Indefinite articles a and an do not refer to a particular noun. We choose between them based on the first sound of the following word, not its spelling:

  • Use A before singular countable nouns beginning with a consonant sound (e.g. a cot, a rug).
  • Use AN before singular countable nouns beginning with a vowel sound (e.g. an ostrich).

Exceptions and Silent Letters

Condition Rule Example Explanation
Vowel spelling, consonant sound a university / a European Pronounced with "yu" sound (consonant)
Vowel spelling, "w" sound a one-sided story Pronounced like "wa" (consonant)
Consonant spelling, vowel sound an MLA / an NCC cadet "M" starts with "em" sound; "N" with "en"
Silent H an honest man / an hour / an heir "H" is silent, so word starts with vowel sound

REF_ID: 104_DEFINITE_THE

The Definite Article: The

(•‿•)
Artie is loading...

The definite article the points to someone or something in particular. It is used:

  • When referring to a noun already mentioned (e.g. I bought a car. The car has six airbags.).
  • Before a singular noun representing a whole class (e.g. The octopus has eight tentacles.).
  • Before adjectives used as nouns (e.g. The injured were given first aid.).
  • Before superlative adjectives (e.g. the best athlete, the tallest member).
  • With proper nouns of specific categories (rivers, mountain ranges, holy books, well-known buildings, etc.).

REF_ID: 105_OMISSION

Omission of Articles

(•‿•)
Artie is loading...

Knowing when NOT to use an article is just as important! We omit articles before:

  • Proper Nouns: e.g. Jaipur, Rajasthan.
  • Common Nouns in widest sense: e.g. Children love to play.
  • Abstract Nouns: e.g. Laughter is infectious., Life is beautiful.
  • Material Nouns: e.g. Gold is expensive., bracelet made of silver.
  • Languages: e.g. speak German. (Unless used as adjective: the Swedish language).
  • Meals and Games: e.g. prepared lunch, Cricket is my favourite.
CRITICAL RULE We omit articles before places like hospital, school, college, church, bed, temple, office when referring to their primary purpose. However, if we visit them for a secondary purpose, we use the:
  • Primary: I reach school on time. (to study)
  • Secondary: He has gone to the school to repair furniture. (not to study)
REF_ID: 106_REPETITION_QUANTITY

Repetition & Quantity Rules

(•‿•)
Artie is loading...

1. Repetition of Articles

  • One item, single article: a pink and purple gown (one gown, two colors).
  • Two items, multiple articles: a pink and a purple gown (two separate gowns).
  • Same Person: The actor and director was awarded. (single person, article before first noun).
  • Different Persons: The actor and the director were awarded. (two people, article before both).

2. Few vs. Little

Type Hardly Any (Negative) Small Number / Amount (Positive) All of it (Specific)
Countable Few A few The few
Uncountable Little A little The little
