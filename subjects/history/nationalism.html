Factors Promoting Indian Nationalism

(•‿•)
Loading History-Bot...

1. Economic Exploitation by the British

The systematic economic drain of India served as a massive catalyst for national unity. All classes suffered: peasants lost their lands to revenue collectors, artisans were ruined by cheap British machine goods, and educated youth faced severe discrimination in employment.

2. Socio-Religious Reform Movements

Reformers like **Raja Ram Mohan Roy** (Brahmo Samaj) and **Jyotirao Phule** (Satyashodhak Samaj) worked to eradicate social evils like caste, Sati, and untouchability. These reforms restored self-respect and created a sense of equality and national solidarity among Indians.

3. Western Education & English Language

The introduction of Western education exposed Indians to concepts of **liberty, democracy, and equality**. Crucially, the English language acted as a **common medium of communication** among leaders from different linguistic regions (e.g. Bengal, Bombay, Madras).

4. Role of the Press and Transport

  • Indian newspapers (like Amrita Bazar Patrika and Kesari) exposed colonial abuses and distributed nationalistic ideas.
  • The rapid expansion of the **railways and telegraphs** broke regional isolation, allowing national leaders to meet, coordinate, and hold political campaigns.

Repressive Policies & The Birth of the INC

(•‿•)
Loading History-Bot...

1. Lord Lytton's Repressive Policies (1876–1880)

  • Vernacular Press Act (1878): Confiscated the printing presses of Indian newspapers if they published material critical of British policies. It was a "gagging act" that did not apply to English-language papers.
  • Arms Act (1878): Made it a criminal offense for Indians to carry or own weapons without a license, but **exempted all Europeans** from this rule.
  • Grand Delhi Durbar (1877): Lord Lytton held a lavish imperial durbar to crown Queen Victoria as Empress of India, while South India was suffering under a catastrophic famine. This showcased heartless extravagance.
  • ICS Age Reduction: Reduced the maximum age to sit for the Indian Civil Services exam from 21 to 19, making it nearly impossible for Indian students to compete.

2. The Ilbert Bill Controversy (1883)

Introduced by Lord Ripon's law member, C.P. Ilbert, this bill proposed allowing **Indian magistrates** to try **European offenders** in criminal cases. British residents launched a fierce, racially charged protest against the bill. The British government capitulated and withdrew the bill, teaching Indians that organized political action was the only way to demand rights.

3. Foundation of the Indian National Congress (1885)

  • **Founder**: **Allan Octavian Hume (A.O. Hume)**, a retired British civil servant who wanted a "safety valve" to channel political discontent safely.
  • **First Session**: Held from **28th to 31st December 1885** at Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College, **Bombay**.
  • **President**: **Womesh Chandra Bonnerjee (W.C. Bonnerjee)**. It was attended by 72 delegates from across India.

Key Pre-Congress Associations

Before the INC was formed, early leaders created regional associations that laid the groundwork for national political action:

  • East India Association (1866): Founded in **London** by **Dadabhai Naoroji** to inform the British public and Parliament about Indian grievances and influence policy.
  • Indian Association (1876): Founded in **Calcutta** by **Surendranath Banerjee** and Anandamohan Bose. It was the first major political organization representing educated middle-class interests, organizing protests against the ICS age cuts and Lytton's press acts.
