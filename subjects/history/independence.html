Cabinet Mission & Mountbatten Plan

(•‿•)
Loading History-Bot...

1. The Cabinet Mission Plan (1946)

Sent by British PM Clement Attlee to work out a transfer of power. Its key proposals were:

  • **Federal Union**: A loose federal union consisting of both British Indian provinces and princely states. The Union would handle Foreign Affairs, Defense, and Communications.
  • **Grouping of Provinces**: Provinces were divided into three groups (Group A: Hindu-majority, Group B & C: Muslim-majority) to appease the Muslim League's demand for autonomy.
  • **Constituent Assembly**: To draft a new constitution, with an interim coalition government to run the country.

2. The Mountbatten Plan (3 June 1947)

Viceroy Lord Mountbatten realized that a united India was no longer possible. He proposed:

  • **Partition**: India would be partitioned into two independent dominions—**India** and **Pakistan**.
  • **Boundary Commission**: A Boundary Commission (headed by Sir Cyril Radcliffe) would be set up to demarcate borders in Punjab and Bengal.
  • **Princely States**: They were free to join either India or Pakistan, or remain independent.

3. Why Congress Accepted Partition

The Congress, historically committed to a united India, reluctantly accepted partition because:

  • **To Avoid Civil War**: Communal riots (sparked by Jinnah's *Direct Action Day*) had killed thousands, and refusal to partition would lead to a bloody, nationwide civil war.
  • **Unworkable Interim Government**: The coalition government was completely paralyzed by the Muslim League's obstructionist tactics, making administration impossible.
  • **A Strong Center**: Partition allowed India to have a strong central government rather than a weak, unstable federal union.

The Indian Independence Act 1947

(•‿•)
Loading History-Bot...

1. Key Provisions of the Act

Passed by the British Parliament based on the Mountbatten Plan, the Act defined the final exit of British rule:

  • **Creation of Two Dominions**: Creation of two independent Dominions, **India** and **Pakistan**, with effect from **15 August 1947**.
  • **Governor-General**: Each Dominion would have a Governor-General appointed by the Crown (Lord Mountbatten for India, M.A. Jinnah for Pakistan).
  • **Lapse of Paramountcy**: British paramountcy over the Princely States would lapse. All treaties and agreements would terminate.
  • **Constituent Assembly as Sovereign**: The Constituent Assemblies of both Dominions were given complete sovereign powers to draft their own constitutions and pass any laws.
  • **Office of Secretary of State**: The office of the Secretary of State for India was abolished, and his duties were transferred to the Secretary for Commonwealth Affairs.

2. Immediate Consequences

The implementation of the Act resulted in the birth of independent India, but was accompanied by a catastrophic, violent **mass migration** of millions of refugees across newly drawn borders in Punjab and Bengal, presenting independent India with a massive refugee crisis.
