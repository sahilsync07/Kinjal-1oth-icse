Moderate Beliefs & Constitutional Methods

(•‿•)
Loading History-Bot...

1. The Early Nationalist Belief System

  • Faith in British Justice: Moderates had a deep, almost unshakeable faith in the British sense of **honesty, fair play, and justice**. They believed that British rule had brought massive benefits (law and order, railways, Western education) to India.
  • Concept of British Connection: They believed that India's connection with Britain was **providential and beneficial** in the long run. They did not demand immediate, absolute independence, but rather **gradual self-government** within the British Empire.
  • Targeting Lack of Awareness: They believed the main problem was not British rule itself, but the **lack of awareness** in England about the real grievances of Indians. They felt that if the British public and Parliament were properly informed, all demands would be granted.

2. The "Three Ps" Constitutional Methods

To secure reforms, they strictly avoided mass protests or civil disobedience, relying exclusively on three constitutional tools:

  1. Petitions: Drafting detailed, legal petitions to high-level British officials and the Viceroy.
  2. Prayers: Formally requesting administrative and legislative updates at annual sessions.
  3. Protests: Holding peaceful public meetings, writing articles in newspapers, and passing resolutions in the Congress.

3. Key Administrative Demands

  • **Legislative**: Expansion and reform of Legislative Councils (more elected Indian members).
  • **Administrative**: Separation of the Judiciary from the Executive, and holding the **Indian Civil Service (ICS) examinations simultaneously in India and England** (so Indian candidates did not have to travel to London).
  • **Economic**: Reduction of excessive land revenue, reduction of military expenditure, and tax concessions for local industries.

Prominent Moderate Leaders & Contributions

(•‿•)
Loading History-Bot...

1. Dadabhai Naoroji ("Grand Old Man of India")

  • Formulated the monumental **Drain of Wealth Theory** in his book *Poverty and Un-British Rule in India*. He mathematically demonstrated how India's wealth was being systematically drained to Britain without economic returns.
  • Founded the **East India Association** in London (1866) to influence British public opinion.
  • Thrice elected as President of the Indian National Congress (1886, 1893, 1906).

2. Gopal Krishna Gokhale ("Political Guru of Gandhi")

  • Founded the **Servants of India Society** in 1905 to train patriotic, dedicated workers to devote their lives to social reforms and serving the country.
  • As a member of the Imperial Legislative Council, he strongly criticized high military spending and heavy land taxes, presenting detailed and convincing budgets.

3. Surendranath Banerjee ("The Indian Burke")

  • Founded the **Indian Association** in 1876 in Calcutta to unite middle-class intellectuals.
  • Convened the **National Conference** in 1883, which merged with the INC in 1886, showing early organizational foresight.
  • Twice elected INC President; led the early Swadeshi agitation against Bengal Partition.

4. Outstanding Contributions of the Moderates

  • Created National Consciousness: They educated Indians in political work and created a strong, unified national sentiment across regional boundaries.
  • Exposed Economic Exploitation: Through the Drain of Wealth theory, they proved to the masses that British policies were impoverishing India, breaking the myth of British benevolence.
  • Laid the Foundation: They established a highly organized, constitutional launchpad for all subsequent national mass struggles led by Tilak, Bose, and Gandhi.

Historical Context of the 1907 Surat Split

By 1905, a younger faction within the INC (the **Assertive Nationalists** or Radicals) became increasingly frustrated with the "begging" and petitional methods of the Moderates. They felt that constitutional prayers were ineffective against Lytton's and Curzon's imperial policies. This ideological friction culminated in the historic **Surat Split of 1907**, where the Congress split into two distinct factions.
