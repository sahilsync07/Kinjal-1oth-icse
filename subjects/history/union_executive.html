The Union Executive of India comprises the President, the Vice-President, the Prime Minister, and the Council of Ministers. It is responsible for implementing the laws enacted by the Parliament.

The President of India

(•‿•)
Loading Civics-Bot...

1. Qualifications, Election & Term

  • Qualifications: Must be a citizen of India, at least 35 years of age, and qualified to be elected as a member of the Lok Sabha. Must not hold any office of profit.
  • Indirect Election: Elected by an Electoral College consisting of:
    • Elected members of both Houses of Parliament (Lok Sabha & Rajya Sabha).
    • Elected members of the Legislative Assemblies (MLAs) of the States.
  • Term & Impeachment: Serves for 5 years. Can be removed earlier only through **Impeachment** for 'Violation of the Constitution' (Article 61), initiated by either House of Parliament with a 2/3rd majority.

2. Powers of the President

  • Executive Powers: All executive business is run in his name. Appoints the Prime Minister, Council of Ministers, Governors, Chief Justice, and Ambassadors.
  • Legislative Powers: Summons and prorogues Parliament. Can dissolve the Lok Sabha on PM's advice. Addresses Parliament. Promulgates **Ordinances** when Parliament is not in session.
  • Financial Powers: Money Bills can only be introduced in the Lok Sabha with the President's prior recommendation. Controls the Contingency Fund of India.
  • Emergency Powers: Can declare three types of emergencies:
    • National Emergency (Art 352): Due to war or external aggression.
    • State Emergency / President's Rule (Art 356): Breakdown of constitutional machinery in a state.
    • Financial Emergency (Art 360): Threat to financial stability.

Prime Minister & Cabinet

(•‿•)
Loading Civics-Bot...

1. The Prime Minister

  • Appointment: Appointed by the President. The President *must* appoint the leader of the majority party or coalition in the Lok Sabha.
  • Powers & Role: Link between President and Cabinet. Head of the Council of Ministers. Allocates portfolios to ministers. Decides policy directions of the nation.

2. Council of Ministers vs Cabinet

Council of MinistersThe Cabinet
A large body consisting of 60 to 80 ministers (all ranks).A small, cohesive inner body of 15 to 20 senior ministers.
Includes Cabinet Ministers, Ministers of State, and Deputy Ministers.Consists of Cabinet Ministers only, holding key portfolios (Home, Finance, Defense).
Rarely meets as a whole body.Meets regularly to determine national policy and execute choices.

3. Principles of Ministerial Responsibility

  • Collective Responsibility: The ministers sink or swim together. If a **No-Confidence Motion** is passed against even a single minister in the Lok Sabha, the *entire Council of Ministers must resign* together.
  • Individual Responsibility: Each minister is individually responsible to the President, meaning the President can dismiss a minister on the advice of the Prime Minister.

ICSE Give-Reason Masterclass

Scenario / QuestionConstitutional / Political Reason
Why is the President of India called a nominal head?Because India follows a parliamentary system of democracy where the real executive power is vested in the Prime Minister and the Cabinet. The President acts only on their aid and advice.
Why can a Money Bill not be introduced in the Rajya Sabha?Because the Lok Sabha consists of directly elected representatives of the people who hold supreme authority over the public purse and taxation.
Why must the entire Council of Ministers resign if a No-Confidence motion is passed?Due to the principle of **Collective Responsibility**. The Council of Ministers functions as a single unit, and loss of confidence in one senior policy decision represents a loss of confidence in the entire team.
Why is an Ordinance called a temporary law?Because it is promulgated by the President during recess of Parliament. It ceases to operate within 6 weeks of the reassembly of Parliament unless approved by both Houses.
