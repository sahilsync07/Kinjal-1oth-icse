The Judiciary of India is a single, integrated system of courts that interprets the laws and administers justice. Unlike other federal nations, India has a single unified judicial hierarchy headed by the Supreme Court.

The Supreme Court

(•‿•)
Loading Judiciary-Bot...

1. Composition & Independence

  • Qualifications: Must be a citizen of India, must have been a judge of a High Court for at least 5 years, or an advocate of a High Court for at least 10 years, or a distinguished jurist in the opinion of the President.
  • Independence of Judiciary: To ensure unbiased choices:
    • Security of Tenure: Judges serve until 65 years of age. Removed only via a highly complex impeachment address passed by both Houses.
    • Consolidated Fund: Salaries are charged directly to the Consolidated Fund of India and cannot be voted upon.
    • No Conduct Discussion: Conduct of judges cannot be discussed in Parliament except during impeachment.

2. Jurisdictions of the Supreme Court

  • Original Jurisdiction: Cases that can be brought *directly* to the Supreme Court. Covers disputes between:
    • Government of India and one or more States.
    • Two or more States.
    • Enforcement of Fundamental Rights (Writ Jurisdiction under Art 32).
  • Appellate Jurisdiction: Hears appeals against decisions of the High Courts in Constitutional, Civil, or Criminal cases.
  • Advisory Jurisdiction: The President can seek the Supreme Court's opinion on any question of law or fact of public importance.
  • Court of Record: All its judgments are recorded for perpetual memory and testimony, acting as legal precedents. Can punish for its own **Contempt**.
  • Judicial Review: Power to declare any law passed by Parliament or State Assemblies null and void if it violates the Constitution.

High Courts & Subordinate Courts

(•‿•)
Loading Judiciary-Bot...

1. The High Courts

  • The highest court of justice in a State. Judges retire at **62 years** of age.
  • Jurisdiction: Holds Original and Appellate jurisdiction. Issue writs under Article 226 for enforcement of fundamental or other legal rights. Holds power of superintendence over all subordinate courts in the state.

2. Subordinate Courts

  • District Court (Civil): Heads civil disputes (wills, property, contracts). Headed by the District Judge.
  • Sessions Court (Criminal): Hears criminal cases (murder, theft, robbery). Headed by the Sessions Judge. Can award death sentence (which must be confirmed by the High Court).

3. Lok Adalats (People's Courts)

  • Meaning: Alternative dispute resolution bodies where pending disputes are settled amicably outside formal courts.
  • Advantages:
    • Fast & Inexpensive: No court fees. Direct interaction with judges without lawyers.
    • Compromise: Decisions are final, binding, and cannot be appealed, preventing future litigation loops.
    • De-congestion: Relieves the massive backlog of cases in formal judiciary channels.

ICSE Give-Reason Masterclass

Scenario / QuestionConstitutional / Judicial Reason
Why is the Indian Judiciary called integrated?Because all courts in India represent a single continuous chain of appeal. The Supreme Court stands at the apex, and its decisions are legally binding on all courts down to subordinate levels.
Why are Supreme Court judges salaries paid from the Consolidated Fund?To ensure the independence of the judiciary. Since their salaries are not voted upon by the Parliament, judges are free from any legislative pressure or financial influence.
Why does a Sessions Court death sentence require High Court confirmation?To prevent miscarriage of justice. Capital punishment is irreversible, so a higher review ensures the trial was absolutely fair and legally sound.
Why is the Supreme Court called a Court of Record?Because all its judgments, trials, and decisions are preserved for permanent testimony and reference. They are cited as legal precedents by lower courts, and their truth cannot be questioned.
Why are Lok Adalats growing in popularity?Because they resolve petty disputes instantly without court fees, legal costs, or complex civil codes, saving the common citizen years of courtroom litigation.
