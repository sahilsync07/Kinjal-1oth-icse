Political, Economic & Social Causes

(•‿•)
Loading History-Bot...

1. Political Causes

  • Doctrine of Lapse: Lord Dalhousie's policy annexed states where the native ruler died without a natural male heir. Adopted sons were refused recognition (e.g., Jhansi, Satara, Nagpur). Nana Saheb, adoptive son of Peshwa Baji Rao II, was refused his pension.
  • Annexation of Awadh (1856): Annexed on the pretext of **"maladministration/misgovernment"**. This outraged the populace and sepoys since Nawab Wajid Ali Shah was highly popular, and Awadh had been a loyal British ally.
  • Disrespect to Bahadur Shah Zafar: The British declared that after Bahadur Shah, his successors could not use the imperial title of 'King' and would have to vacate the Red Fort.

2. Economic Causes

  • Ruin of Handicrafts: Duty-free British machine-made goods flooded Indian markets while heavy tariffs were placed on Indian handloom exports.
  • Drain of Wealth: Continuous export of Indian raw materials and taxes to Britain without economic returns.
  • Peasant Distress: Excessive land revenues under the Ryotwari and Mahalwari systems forced farmers to borrow from moneylenders, leading to land confiscation.

3. Socio-Religious Causes

  • Social Reforms Interference: Laws like Sati Abolition (1829) and Widow Remarriage Act (1856) were viewed by orthodox Indians as threats to custom.
  • Christian Missionary Activities: The British government allowed mass proselytization, and the Religious Disabilities Act allowed converts to inherit ancestral property.

Military Causes & The Immediate Spark

(•‿•)
Loading History-Bot...

1. Military Causes (Sepoy Grievances)

  • General Service Enlistment Act (1856): Mandated that Indian recruits must serve wherever required, including **overseas**. Orthodox Hindus believed crossing the ocean (kala pani) led to loss of caste and social ostracism.
  • Pay Disparity and Ill-treatment: Indian soldiers were paid a fraction of the salary of European soldiers of equivalent rank, and were treated with racial contempt. Promotion was capped (most could not rise above Havildar).
  • Annexation of Awadh military impact: Most Bengal Army sepoys came from Awadh. Annexation meant their families lost tax privileges, turning the sepoys directly against the Company.

2. The Immediate Cause: Greased Cartridges

  • In 1856, the British introduced the new **Enfield Rifle**.
  • The cartridges for this rifle had a greased paper cover that had to be **bitten off** before loading.
  • A rumor spread that the grease was made from **cow and pig fat**.
  • This outraged both **Hindus** (who revere the cow) and **Muslims** (who consider pork forbidden/halal violation).
  • On 29th March 1857, **Mangal Pandey** of the 34th Native Infantry at Barrackpore refused to use the cartridge and shot his British officer, lighting the spark. The formal mutiny began on **10th May 1857 in Meerut**.

Key Consequences of the 1857 Revolt

The revolt failed to overthrow the British, but it fundamentally transformed the administrative architecture of India:

  • End of East India Company Rule: The **Government of India Act 1858** transferred all power directly to the **British Crown**. The Governor-General was given the title of **Viceroy** (acting as direct representative of the Crown).
  • Queen Victoria's Proclamation (1858): Pledged that the British would **respect the treaty rights of native princes**, abandon territorial expansion (ending Doctrine of Lapse), maintain complete religious neutrality, and grant equal status to all citizens.
  • Army Reorganization (Peel Commission): To prevent future mutinies, the ratio of European to Indian soldiers was increased (2:1 in Bengal, 3:1 in Madras/Bombay). Artillery and strategic divisions were kept exclusively in European hands, and recruitment was divided on "martial" and "non-martial" lines (divide and rule).
