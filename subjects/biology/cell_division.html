The Cell Cycle

(•‿•)
Loading Bio-Bot...

The cell cycle is the ordered sequence of events that occur in a cell from one division to the next. It consists of two main phases:

Phase Sub-Phases Key Events
Interphase
(~90-95% of cycle)		 G1 (Gap 1) Cell grows, synthesises proteins & RNA. Prepares for DNA replication.
S (Synthesis) DNA replicates — each chromosome forms two sister chromatids joined at centromere. DNA: 2n → 4n content.
G2 (Gap 2) Cell synthesises tubulin (for spindle fibres), checks for DNA errors, prepares to enter mitosis.
M Phase
(Mitotic Phase)		 Karyokinesis + Cytokinesis Nuclear division (Prophase → Metaphase → Anaphase → Telophase) followed by cytoplasm division.
KEY TERMS Karyokinesis = Division of the nucleus.
Cytokinesis = Division of the cytoplasm.
G0 Phase = Quiescent phase where cells exit the cycle and stop dividing (e.g., neurons, mature RBCs).

Important: Interphase is NOT a "resting phase" — the cell is metabolically very active. It only "rests" from division.

Mitosis: Equational Division

(•‿•)
Loading Bio-Bot...

Mitosis is the division of the nucleus into two genetically identical daughter nuclei. Remember the mnemonic: PMAT — Prophase, Metaphase, Anaphase, Telophase.

Stage Key Events
Prophase
(Longest stage)
  • Chromatin condenses into visible chromosomes (each = 2 sister chromatids joined at centromere).
  • Centrioles migrate to opposite poles (animal cells).
  • Nuclear membrane & nucleolus disappear.
  • Spindle fibres begin to form.
Metaphase
  • Chromosomes align at the equatorial plate (metaphase plate).
  • Spindle fibres attach to centromeres via kinetochores.
  • Chromosomes are most condensed and visible — ideal for karyotyping.
Anaphase
(Shortest stage)
  • Centromere splits — sister chromatids separate.
  • Spindle fibres contract, pulling daughter chromosomes to opposite poles.
  • Chromosomes appear V-shaped or J-shaped as they're pulled.
Telophase
  • Nuclear membrane reforms around each set of chromosomes.
  • Nucleolus reappears; chromosomes uncoil back to chromatin.
  • Spindle fibres disintegrate.

Cytokinesis

Animal Cell Plant Cell
Cleavage furrow — cell membrane pinches inward from outside. Cell plate — Golgi vesicles fuse at centre, growing outward to form new cell wall.
MNEMONIC "Please Meet At Twelve" = Prophase → Metaphase → Anaphase → Telophase

Meiosis: Reduction Division

(•‿•)
Loading Bio-Bot...

Meiosis is a special type of cell division that produces four haploid (n) daughter cells from one diploid (2n) parent cell. It occurs in reproductive (germ) cells to form gametes.

Meiosis I — Reduction Division

Stage Key Events
Prophase I
(Longest & most complex)
  • Synapsis: Homologous chromosomes pair up forming bivalents (tetrads).
  • Crossing over: Non-sister chromatids exchange segments at chiasmata.
  • Nuclear membrane & nucleolus disappear.
Metaphase I Bivalents align at equatorial plate. Independent assortment — random orientation of each bivalent.
Anaphase I Homologous chromosomes separate (NOT sister chromatids). Centromere does NOT split. Chromosome number is halved (2n → n).
Telophase I Nuclear membrane reforms. Two haploid cells formed, each with one chromosome from each homologous pair.

Meiosis II — Equational Division (Similar to Mitosis)

Stage Key Events
Prophase II Chromosomes condense again. No synapsis or crossing over this time.
Metaphase II Chromosomes align at equatorial plate (like mitosis).
Anaphase II Centromere splits — sister chromatids separate and move to opposite poles.
Telophase II Nuclear membrane reforms. Four haploid (n) daughter cells result.
KEY DIFFERENCE In Anaphase I: Homologous chromosomes separate (centromere intact).
In Anaphase II: Sister chromatids separate (centromere splits) — just like mitotic anaphase.

Mitosis vs Meiosis

Feature Mitosis Meiosis
Also called Equational division Reduction division
No. of divisions One Two (Meiosis I & II)
Daughter cells 2 diploid (2n) cells 4 haploid (n) cells
Genetic result Identical to parent Genetically different
Crossing over Does NOT occur Occurs in Prophase I
Synapsis Does NOT occur Occurs in Prophase I
Occurs in Somatic (body) cells Germ (reproductive) cells
Purpose Growth, repair, regeneration Gamete formation, genetic variation

Plant Cell vs Animal Cell Division

Feature Plant Cell Animal Cell
Centrioles Absent (in higher plants) Present
Spindle type Anastral (no asters) Astral (with asters)
Cytokinesis Cell plate (centre → outward) Cleavage furrow (outside → inward)
Cell plate source Golgi vesicles Not applicable

Chromosome Structure & Significance of Cell Division

(•‿•)
Loading Bio-Bot...

Chromosome Structure

Part Description
Chromatin Diffuse, uncoiled DNA + histone protein complex visible during interphase.
Chromosome Condensed, tightly coiled chromatin visible during cell division. Made of DNA wrapped around histone proteins.
Centromere Constricted region holding two sister chromatids together. Spindle fibres attach here via kinetochore.
Sister Chromatids Two identical copies of a chromosome formed after DNA replication, joined at centromere.
Telomeres Protective caps at chromosome ends — prevent deterioration and fusion.

Human Chromosome Count

  • Total: 46 chromosomes (23 pairs) — 22 pairs of autosomes + 1 pair of sex chromosomes.
  • Females: 44 + XX = 46
  • Males: 44 + XY = 46
  • Gametes: 23 chromosomes (haploid) — 22 autosomes + 1 sex chromosome.

Significance of Cell Division

Type Significance
Mitosis
  • Growth — increases cell number in multicellular organisms.
  • Repair — replaces worn-out or damaged cells (e.g., skin, blood).
  • Regeneration — regrows lost parts (e.g., lizard's tail, starfish arm).
  • Asexual reproduction — in organisms like Amoeba, Hydra.
  • Maintains genetic stability — identical DNA in all body cells.
Meiosis
  • Gamete formation — produces haploid sperm and ova.
  • Maintains chromosome constancy across generations.
  • Introduces genetic variation (crossing over + independent assortment).
  • Essential for evolution and natural selection.
BOARD EXAM FAVOURITE "What would happen if meiosis did not occur?" — Without meiosis, gametes would be diploid. Fusion of two diploid gametes would double the chromosome number every generation, leading to abnormalities.
