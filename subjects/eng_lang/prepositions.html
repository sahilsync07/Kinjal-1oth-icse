Basic, Time & Directional Prepositions

(•‿•)

1. What is a Preposition?

A preposition is a word placed before a noun or pronoun to show its relation to some other word in the sentence (e.g., The book is on the table).

2. Time Prepositions

  • At: Used for a specific point in time or clock time (e.g., at 5:00 PM, at noon, at midnight).
  • On: Used for days of the week and specific dates (e.g., on Monday, on 15th August).
  • In: Used for non-specific times during a day, month, season, year, or century (e.g., in the morning, in December, in summer, in 2027).
  • For vs. Since:
    • For: Indicates a period or duration of time (e.g., for three hours, for ten years).
    • Since: Indicates a specific starting point in time (e.g., since 10:00 AM, since yesterday, since 2020).

3. Place & Position Prepositions

  • At vs. In:
    • At: Indicates a specific point or location (e.g., at the bus stop, at the entrance).
    • In: Indicates that something is enclosed or within a 3D boundary (e.g., in the room, in India).
  • Between vs. Among:
    • Between: Used when referring to relations or divisions involving exactly two entities (e.g., between Ram and Hari).
    • Among: Used when referring to divisions or relations involving three or more entities (e.g., among the students).
  • Beside vs. Besides:
    • Beside: Means 'next to' or 'at the side of' (e.g., Sit beside me).
    • Besides: Means 'in addition to' or 'except' (e.g., Besides physics, she loves math).

4. Movement & Direction

  • To vs. Into:
    • To: Shows destination or direction of travel (e.g., We went to school).
    • Into: Indicates entry or movement towards the interior of a space (e.g., She ran into the building).
  • On vs. Onto:
    • On: Represents a static position on a surface (e.g., The cat is on the table).
    • Onto: Represents movement landing on a surface (e.g., The cat jumped onto the table).

Fixed Prepositions & Phrasal Verbs

1. Fixed (Appropriate) Prepositions

Certain words (verbs, nouns, or adjectives) are always followed by specific, fixed prepositions. There is no logical rule for this; they must be memorized.

WordPrepositionExample Usage
abidebyHe must abide by the decision.
accuseofShe was accused of theft.
addictedtoHe is addicted to gaming.
congratulateonCongratulate her on her success.
dealwith / inDeal with a crisis // Deal in silks.
angrywith / atAngry with a person // Angry at actions.

2. Common Phrasal Verbs

A phrasal verb is a combination of a verb and a preposition or adverb that creates a meaning different from the original verb.

  • Bring up: To raise or rear a child (e.g., Brought up by grandparents).
  • Put out: To extinguish a fire (e.g., Put out the flames).
  • Take after: To resemble an older relative (e.g., She takes after her mother).
  • Call off: To cancel an event (e.g., The match was called off).
  • Turn down: To reject or refuse an offer (e.g., Turn down the proposal).
  • Look up: To search for information in a reference book (e.g., Look up the spelling).
