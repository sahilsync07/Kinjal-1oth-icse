Mastering the 12 Tense Forms

(•‿•)
Artie is loading...

Tense refers to the form of a verb that indicates the time of an action. There are three main times—Present, Past, and Future—each divided into four aspects:

Aspect Present Past Future
Simple Subject + V1 (-s/-es)
He writes a letter. (Habits/truths)		 Subject + V2
He wrote a letter. (Completed past)		 Subject + will/shall + V1
He will write a letter. (Future plan)
Continuous Subject + is/am/are + V-ing
He is writing a letter. (Ongoing now)		 Subject + was/were + V-ing
He was writing a letter. (Ongoing then)		 Subject + will be + V-ing
He will be writing a letter. (Ongoing future)
Perfect Subject + has/have + V3
He has written a letter. (Finished just now)		 Subject + had + V3
He had written a letter. (Done before past point)		 Subject + will have + V3
He will have written a letter. (Done by future point)
Perfect Cont. Subject + has/have been + V-ing
He has been writing since 4 PM.		 Subject + had been + V-ing
He had been writing for hours.		 Subject + will have been + V-ing
He will have been writing.
CRITICAL TIME MARKERS
  • Present Perfect + since/for: Indicates action started in past and continues to now.
  • Past Perfect + when/before + Simple Past: Earlier action takes Past Perfect. (e.g. The train had left before I reached.)
  • Future Perfect + by/by the time: Action completed by a specific future point.

Sequence of Tenses

(•‿•)
Artie is loading...

The Sequence of Tenses is the principle that dictates the tense in a subordinate clause based on the tense of the principal (main) clause:

Rule 1: Past Principal Clause → Past Subordinate Clause

If the main verb is in the past tense, verbs in subordinate clauses must also be in the past tense.

  • He said that he would come. (NOT will)
  • I knew that she was studying. (NOT is)
EXCEPTION If the subordinate clause states a universal/habitual truth or proverb, keep it in the Simple Present:
The teacher said that the Earth revolves around the Sun. (NOT revolved)

Rule 2: Conjunctions of Purpose

Conjunction Main Clause Tense Subordinate Clause Structure
so that / in order that Present / Future Uses may + verb (e.g. We study so that we may pass.)
so that / in order that Past Uses might + verb (e.g. We studied so that we might pass.)
lest Any Tense Strictly followed by should (e.g. Run fast lest you should miss the train.)
