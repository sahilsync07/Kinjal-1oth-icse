Part 1: The Desire for Education

(•‿•)
Artie is loading...

Summary: The story revolves around a young girl named Rebati from a conservative rural Odia family. Her father, Shyamabandhu Mohanty, is a record-keeper (Gumasta). Rebati strongly desires to study, which was taboo for women at the time. A young, progressive teacher named Basudeb arrives in the village and starts teaching her.

Explanation: Fakir Mohan Senapati uses this sub-part to critique the patriarchal and superstitious mindset of 19th-century rural Odisha. Rebati's grandmother fiercely opposes her education, believing that girls who read bring bad luck to the family.

Part 2: The Cholera Epidemic

(•‿•)
Artie is loading...

Summary: Tragedy strikes the village in the form of a devastating Cholera (ହଇଜା) epidemic. Rebati's parents both succumb to the disease. Shortly after, the kind teacher Basudeb also dies of Cholera. Rebati is left orphaned and heartbroken with her cruel grandmother.

Explanation: The grandmother blames Rebati's education for the deaths, reinforcing blind superstition. The story ends on a deeply tragic note as Rebati herself dies of starvation and grief, repeatedly calling out for her lost loved ones. It is a powerful commentary on how ignorance and disease destroy innocent lives.
