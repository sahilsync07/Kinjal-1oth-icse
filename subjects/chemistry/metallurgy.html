1. Minerals vs. Ores

  • Minerals: Naturally occurring inorganic compounds of metals found in the earth's crust.
  • Ores: Those minerals from which a metal can be **profitably and easily extracted** (e.g. Bauxite is the ore of Aluminium). *Rule:* All ores are minerals, but all minerals are not ores.

2. Common Ores to Memorize

  • Aluminium: Bauxite ($Al_2O_3 \cdot 2H_2O$), Cryolite ($Na_3AlF_6$).
  • Iron: Hematite ($Fe_2O_3$).
  • Zinc: Zinc Blende ($ZnS$), Calamine ($ZnCO_3$).

3. Baeyer's Process (Concentrating Bauxite)

Bauxite contains impurities like ferric oxide ($Fe_2O_3$) and silica ($SiO_2$). It is purified chemically:

  1. Bauxite is heated with concentrated $NaOH$ at $150^\circ\text{C}$ to form soluble Sodium Aluminate:
    $Al_2O_3 \cdot 2H_2O + 2NaOH \rightarrow 2NaAlO_2 + 3H_2O$. Impurities remain insoluble and are filtered out.
  2. Sodium Aluminate is diluted and agitated to precipitate Aluminium Hydroxide:
    $NaAlO_2 + 2H_2O \rightarrow Al(OH)_3\downarrow + NaOH$.
  3. $Al(OH)_3$ precipitate is filtered, washed, and ignited at $1000^\circ\text{C}$ to yield pure Alumina:
    $2Al(OH)_3 \xrightarrow{1000^\circ\text{C}} Al_2O_3 + 3H_2O$.

1. Hall-Heroult Electrolytic Reduction

Pure alumina ($Al_2O_3$) has a very high melting point ($2050^\circ\text{C}$) and is a poor conductor. It is electrolyzed in a molten mixture:

  • Electrolyte Mixture: $20\%$ Alumina ($Al_2O_3$), $60\%$ Cryolite ($Na_3AlF_6$), and $20\%$ Fluorspar ($CaF_2$). Powdered coke is sprinkled on the surface to prevent heat loss and oxidation of graphite anodes.
  • Cell Setup: Steel tank lined with carbon acts as the **Cathode**. Graphite rods dipped in the molten electrolyte act as the **Anode**.
  • Electrode Reactions:
    At Cathode: $Al^{3+} + 3e^- \rightarrow Al$ (Molten aluminium sinks to the bottom).
    At Anode: $O^{2-} - 2e^- \rightarrow O$. Oxygen atoms combine to form $O_2$ gas, which reacts with graphite rods, slowly burning them away as $CO_2$ (requiring periodic replacement).

2. Compositions and Uses of Alloys

  • Duralumin ($95\% Al$, $4\% Cu$, $0.5\% Mg$, $0.5\% Mn$): Light, strong, resistant to corrosion. Used in aircraft frames.
  • Brass ($60-80\% Cu$, $20-40\% Zn$): Malleable, decorative, corrosion-resistant. Used in utensils, condenser tubes.
  • Bronze ($80\% Cu$, $18\% Sn$, $2\% Zn$): Tough, highly ductile. Used in statues, medals.
  • Solder ($50\% Pb$, $50\% Sn$): Low melting point. Used to join electrical wires.
