Gay-Lussac's & Avogadro's Laws

1. Gay-Lussac's Law of Combining Volumes

When gases react, they do so in **volumes which bear a simple whole-number ratio to one another**, and to the volumes of gaseous products formed, provided all volumes are measured at the **same temperature and pressure**.

  • Example reaction: $N_2(g) + 3H_2(g) \rightarrow 2NH_3(g)$
  • The volumes of nitrogen, hydrogen, and ammonia are in a simple whole-number ratio of **$1 : 3 : 2$**.

2. Avogadro's Law

Equal volumes of **all gases** under the same conditions of temperature and pressure contain **equal number of molecules**.

  • This explains why volume ratios in a balanced gaseous chemical reaction correspond directly to the molecular ratios (coefficients).
  • Atomicity: The number of atoms present in one molecule of an element (e.g. $O_2$ is diatomic, $O_3$ is triatomic).

1. Defining the Mole

A mole is the amount of substance containing Avogadro's number ($6.022 \times 10^{23}$) of particles.
One mole of any gas at standard temperature and pressure (S.T.P., $0^\circ\text{C}$ and $1\text{ atm}$) occupies **$22.4\text{ dm}^3$ (or litres)**.

2. Vapor Density (V.D.) and Molecular Mass

Vapor density is the ratio of the mass of a certain volume of a gas to the mass of an equal volume of hydrogen under identical conditions.

Relative Molecular Mass = 2 × Vapor Density (VD)

3. Solving Stoichiometry (Mass-Mass & Mass-Volume)

  • Write the balanced equation.
  • Write down the atomic weights / molecular masses of the relevant substances beneath them.
  • Convert given parameters (grams or litres) to moles, or apply the unitary method directly.
    Example: $CaCO_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} CaO + CO_2$.
    $100\text{g } CaCO_3$ yields $22.4\text{L } CO_2$ at S.T.P.
