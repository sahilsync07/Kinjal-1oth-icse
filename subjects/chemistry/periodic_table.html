1. What is Periodicity?

Periodicity refers to the recurrence of similar chemical and physical properties of elements at regular intervals when elements are arranged in increasing order of their atomic numbers. This is caused by the **repetition of similar valence shell electronic configurations**.

2. Two Primary Governing Factors

  • Number of Shells: More shells mean the valence electrons are further from the nucleus, increasing atomic size and reducing nuclear pull on outer electrons.
  • Nuclear Charge: The number of protons in the nucleus. A higher nuclear charge exerts a stronger attractive pull on the electrons, pulling them closer and decreasing atomic size.

3. Atomic Size (Atomic Radius) Trends

  • Across a Period (Left to Right): **Decreases**. As you move across, the number of shells remains the same, but the nuclear charge increases by one at each step. This stronger pull draws the outer shells closer, reducing the size.
  • Down a Group (Top to Bottom): **Increases**. At each step down a group, a new electron shell is added, which overpowers the increasing nuclear charge, pushing valence electrons further away.

1. Ionization Potential (IP) / Ionization Energy

The minimum energy required to remove the most loosely bound valence electron from an isolated, gaseous atom to form a positive cation ($M \rightarrow M^+ + e^-$).

  • **Across a Period**: **Increases** (due to smaller atomic size and higher nuclear charge making it harder to pull electrons away).
  • **Down a Group**: **Decreases** (larger size means outer electrons are less tightly bound).

2. Electron Affinity (EA)

The amount of energy released when an electron is added to an isolated, gaseous atom to form a negative anion ($X + e^- \rightarrow X^-$).

  • **Across a Period**: **Increases** (smaller size, stronger nuclear pull to attract a new electron).
  • **Down a Group**: **Decreases** (larger size, weaker pull on incoming electron).

3. Electronegativity (EN)

The tendency of an atom in a covalent compound to attract the shared pair of electrons toward itself.

  • **Across a Period**: **Increases** (stronger nuclear pull).
  • **Down a Group**: **Decreases** (outer shells are further away, weakening pull).
