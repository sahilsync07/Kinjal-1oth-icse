Acids, Bases, & The pH Scale

1. What are Acids and Bases?

  • Acids: Substances that release hydronium ($H_3O^+$) or hydrogen ($H^+$) ions in aqueous solution (e.g. $HCl \rightarrow H^+ + Cl^-$). Basicity is the number of replaceable $H^+$ ions per molecule (e.g., $HCl$ is monobasic, $H_2SO_4$ is dibasic, $H_3PO_4$ is tribasic).
  • Bases: Oxides or hydroxides of metals that react with hydronium ions to form salt and water only. Soluble bases are called **alkalis** (e.g., $NaOH$, $KOH$). Acidity is the number of replaceable $OH^-$ ions per molecule.

2. The pH Scale (Power of Hydrogen)

pH measures the concentration of $H^+$ ions in solution, ranging from 0 to 14:

  • **pH < 7**: Acidic (lower pH means stronger acid).
  • **pH = 7**: Neutral (pure water).
  • **pH > 7**: Alkaline/Basic (higher pH means stronger alkali).

Salts & Methods of Preparation

(•‿•)
Loading Chem-Bot...

1. Classification of Salts

  • Normal Salt: Formed by the **complete replacement** of replaceable hydrogen ions of an acid by a metal or ammonium ion (e.g. $NaCl$, $Na_2SO_4$). They do not contain any replaceable hydrogen.
  • Acid Salt: Formed by the **partial replacement** of replaceable hydrogen ions of a dibasic or tribasic acid by a metal ion (e.g. $NaHSO_4$, $NaHCO_3$). They contain replaceable hydrogen and turn blue litmus red. Monobasic acids ($HCl$, $HNO_3$) **never** form acid salts!
  • Basic Salt: Formed by the partial replacement of hydroxyl ($OH^-$) groups of a diacidic or triacidic base by an acid radical (e.g. $Pb(OH)Cl$, $Cu(OH)CO_3$).

2. Key Methods of Preparation

  • Neutralization (Direct titration): Soluble salt prep by reacting a soluble alkali and soluble acid (e.g. $NaOH + HCl \rightarrow NaCl + H_2O$).
  • Double Decomposition (Precipitation): Insoluble salt prep by mixing two soluble salt solutions (e.g. $BaCl_2 (aq) + Na_2SO_4 (aq) \rightarrow BaSO_4 \downarrow (ppt) + 2NaCl (aq)$).
  • Direct Combination (Synthesis): Binary anhydrous salts by directly heating a metal and non-metal (e.g. $2Fe + 3Cl_2 \rightarrow 2FeCl_3$).
