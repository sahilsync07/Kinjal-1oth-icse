1. Action of Sodium Hydroxide ($NaOH$)

When $NaOH$ is added dropwise and then in excess to metal salt solutions, insoluble metal hydroxides precipitate out with distinct colors:

  • Calcium ($Ca^{2+}$): White precipitate of $Ca(OH)_2$, **sparingly soluble** in excess.
  • Iron(II) ($Fe^{2+}$): Dirty green precipitate of $Fe(OH)_2$, **insoluble** in excess.
  • Iron(III) ($Fe^{3+}$): Reddish-brown precipitate of $Fe(OH)_3$, **insoluble** in excess.
  • Copper ($Cu^{2+}$): Pale blue precipitate of $Cu(OH)_2$, **insoluble** in excess.
  • Zinc ($Zn^{2+}$): Gelatinous white precipitate of $Zn(OH)_2$, **soluble** in excess (forms soluble sodium zincate: $Na_2ZnO_2$).
  • Lead ($Pb^{2+}$): Chalky white precipitate of $Pb(OH)_2$, **soluble** in excess (forms soluble sodium plumbite: $Na_2PbO_2$).

2. Action of Ammonium Hydroxide ($NH_4OH$)

  • Calcium ($Ca^{2+}$): **No precipitate** is formed because the concentration of $OH^-$ ions from weak $NH_4OH$ is too low to exceed the solubility product of Calcium Hydroxide.
  • Copper ($Cu^{2+}$): Pale blue precipitate, **soluble in excess** to form a **deep inky blue solution** of tetramminecopper(II) sulfate ($[Cu(NH_3)_4]SO_4$).
  • Zinc ($Zn^{2+}$): Gelatinous white precipitate, **soluble in excess** to form a colorless solution of tetramminezinc(II) sulfate.
  • Lead ($Pb^{2+}$): Chalky white precipitate, **insoluble** in excess.

1. What does Amphoteric Mean?

Amphoteric substances are oxides and hydroxides of certain metals (such as **Zinc, Aluminium, and Lead**) that react with **both acids and strong alkalis** to form salt and water. They exhibit both acidic and basic properties.

2. Key Amphoteric Oxides to Note

  • Zinc Oxide ($ZnO$) and Zinc Hydroxide ($Zn(OH)_2$).
  • Aluminium Oxide ($Al_2O_3$) and Aluminium Hydroxide ($Al(OH)_3$).
  • Lead Oxide ($PbO$) and Lead Hydroxide ($Pb(OH)_2$).

3. Key Reactions with Strong Alkalis ($NaOH$, $KOH$)

  • Zinc Oxide: Reacts with hot concentrated $NaOH$ to yield Sodium Zincate.
    Equation: $ZnO + 2NaOH \rightarrow Na_2ZnO_2 + H_2O$.
  • Aluminium Oxide: Reacts with hot concentrated $NaOH$ to yield Sodium Aluminate.
    Equation: $Al_2O_3 + 2NaOH \rightarrow 2NaAlO_2 + H_2O$.
  • Lead Oxide: Reacts with hot concentrated $NaOH$ to yield Sodium Plumbite.
    Equation: $PbO + 2NaOH \rightarrow Na_2PbO_2 + H_2O$.
