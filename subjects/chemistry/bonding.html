Ionic & Covalent Bonding

(•‿•)
Loading Chem-Bot...

1. Ionic (Electrovalent) Bonding

Formed by the **complete transfer of one or more electrons** from a metallic atom to a non-metallic atom, resulting in electrostatic attraction between positive cations and negative anions.

  • **Examples**: $NaCl$ (Sodium Chloride: $Na \cdot + \cdot \ddot{Cl}: \rightarrow Na^+[:\ddot{Cl}:]^-$) and $MgCl_2$ (Magnesium Chloride).
  • **Properties**: High melting/boiling points, soluble in water, insoluble in organic solvents, and conduct electricity in **molten or aqueous state** (due to free mobile ions).

2. Covalent (Molecular) Bonding

Formed by the **mutual sharing of one or more pairs of electrons** between non-metallic atoms to achieve stable configurations.

  • **Examples**: $H_2O$ (Water - single covalent bonds with two lone pairs on oxygen) and $NH_3$ (Ammonia - three single covalent bonds with one lone pair on nitrogen).
  • **Properties**: Low melting/boiling points, insoluble in water (mostly), soluble in organic solvents, and do **not** conduct electricity (due to lack of ions/charged particles).

Coordinate Bonding & Lone Pairs

(•‿•)
Loading Chem-Bot...

1. What is a Coordinate Bond?

A type of covalent bond in which **both shared electrons are contributed by a single atom** (donor). The atom that accepts the pair is the acceptor. It is denoted by a coordinate arrow ($\rightarrow$) pointing from donor to acceptor.

2. Conditions for Coordinate Bonding

  • One of the combining atoms must have **at least one lone pair** of electrons (unshared pair, like in $H_2O$ or $NH_3$).
  • The other combining atom must be **deficient in electrons** (like a hydrogen ion, $H^+$).

3. Key Structures to Master

  • Ammonium Ion ($NH_4^+$): Formed when ammonia ($NH_3$ with 1 lone pair on $N$) donates its lone pair to a hydrogen ion ($H^+$).
    Equation: $NH_3 + H^+ \rightarrow [NH_3 \rightarrow H]^+$ or $NH_4^+$.
  • Hydronium Ion ($H_3O^+$): Formed when a water molecule ($H_2O$ with 2 lone pairs on $O$) donates one lone pair to a hydrogen ion ($H^+$).
    Equation: $H_2O + H^+ \rightarrow [H_2O \rightarrow H]^+$ or $H_3O^+$.
