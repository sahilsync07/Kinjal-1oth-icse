1. Electrolytes vs. Non-Electrolytes

  • Electrolytes: Compounds which in molten state or aqueous solution conduct electricity and undergo chemical decomposition (e.g. $NaCl$, dilute $HCl$, $CuSO_4$). They conduct due to **free mobile ions**.
  • Non-Electrolytes: Compounds which do not conduct electricity in molten or aqueous state (e.g. alcohol, sugar solution, distilled water). They consist of **molecules**, not ions.
  • Strong vs. Weak: Strong electrolytes dissociate completely into ions (e.g. $NaOH$, $H_2SO_4$), while weak electrolytes dissociate only partially (e.g. $CH_3COOH$, $NH_4OH$), containing both ions and molecules.

2. Electrolytic Cell Reactions

  • At Cathode (Negative Electrode): Attracts positive cations. Cations gain electrons (undergo **reduction**) to form neutral atoms.
    Equation: $M^{n+} + ne^- \rightarrow M$
  • At Anode (Positive Electrode): Attracts negative anions. Anions lose electrons (undergo **oxidation**) to form neutral atoms/radicals.
    Equation: $A^{n-} - ne^- \rightarrow A$

1. Selective Discharge of Ions

When multiple ions are present, the ion that is **lower in the electrochemical series** is discharged preferentially at the electrode.
Cation series: $Na^+ < Ca^{2+} < ... < H^+ < Cu^{2+} < Ag^+$ (Silver discharges easiest).
Anion series: $SO_4^{2-} < NO_3^- < Cl^- < Br^- < I^- < OH^-$ (Hydroxide discharges easiest).

2. Electrolysis of Copper Sulfate ($CuSO_4$)

  • With Inert Electrodes (Platinum/Carbon): $Cu^{2+}$ deposits at cathode. $OH^-$ discharges at anode releasing $O_2$ gas. Solution turns acidic due to remaining $H^+$ and $SO_4^{2-}$.
  • With Active Copper Electrodes: $Cu^{2+}$ deposits at cathode. At anode, copper atoms lose electrons and dissolve ($Cu \rightarrow Cu^{2+} + 2e^-$). The blue color of the solution remains constant.

3. Electroplating & Electro-Refining

  • Electroplating: Coating a superior metal over an inferior metal object.
    Rules: Object to be plated is made the **Cathode**. Pure plating metal is made the **Anode**. Electrolyte must contain **ions of the plating metal**. Use low direct current (D.C.) for a smooth coating.
  • Electro-Refining: Purifying copper. Crude copper is anode; thin pure copper is cathode; acidified $CuSO_4$ is electrolyte.
