Natural Vegetation refers to the plant cover that has grown naturally without human intervention over a long period. In India, it is primarily influenced by rainfall and temperature.

Tropical Evergreen & Deciduous Forests

Tropical Evergreen (Rain) Forests

  • Climate: Heavy rainfall (>200 cm), high temperature (>24°C).
  • Distribution: Western slopes of Western Ghats, NE India (Assam, Meghalaya), Andaman & Nicobar.
  • Features: Trees are evergreen (do not shed leaves at the same time), very dense, multi-layered canopy. Hardwood trees.
  • Key Trees: Rosewood, Mahogany, Ebony.

Tropical Deciduous (Monsoon) Forests

  • Climate: Moderate rainfall (100-200 cm), mean temperature (24°C - 27°C).
  • Distribution: Most widespread in India. Covers the Peninsular Plateau and northern plains (UP, Bihar, MP).
  • Features: Trees shed their leaves for 6-8 weeks in dry summer to conserve water. Commercially most exploited.
  • Key Trees: Teak, Sal, Sandalwood (Karnataka), Shisham.
ICSE Give Reason: Why are Tropical Evergreen forests difficult to exploit commercially?
Because they are extremely dense with thick undergrowth, making transport difficult. Also, different species of trees grow tangled together.

Desert, Tidal & Mountain Forests

Tropical Desert (Thorn) Forests

  • Climate: Scanty rainfall (<50 cm).
  • Distribution: Rajasthan, parts of Gujarat and Punjab.
  • Features: Long roots (to tap groundwater), thick fleshy stems, leaves modified into thorns to prevent transpiration.
  • Trees: Babool, Khair, Date Palm, Cactus.

Littoral (Tidal / Mangrove) Forests

  • Location: Deltas of Ganga (Sunderbans), Mahanadi, Godavari, and coastal areas affected by tides.
  • Features: Can survive in both fresh and salt water. Have pneumatophores (breathing roots) that grow upwards to take in oxygen. Have stilt roots for support in soft mud.
  • Trees: Sundari (provides hard, durable boat-building wood), Hintal.

Mountain (Montane) Forests

  • Location: Himalayan region and high altitudes of Nilgiris.
  • Features: Vegetation changes with altitude (deciduous → coniferous → alpine). Coniferous trees have needle-like leaves and cone shapes to let snow slide off.
  • Trees: Pine, Deodar, Silver Fir.

ICSE Give-Reason Masterclass

Scenario / QuestionGeographical Reason
Why do desert plants have deep roots?To tap the deep underground water table since surface water is scarce.
Why do tidal forest trees have breathing roots (pneumatophores)?The waterlogged, marshy saline soil lacks oxygen, so roots grow vertically upwards to breathe.
Why do mountain trees have sloping branches and needle-like leaves?To allow heavy snow to slide off easily without breaking the branches, and needle leaves reduce transpiration.
Why are Deciduous forests commercially most important?They grow in pure stands (same species together), aren't too dense, and yield valuable timber like Teak and Sal.
