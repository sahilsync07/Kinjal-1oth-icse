1. Iron Ore

Iron ore is the foundation of modern industry. India has large reserves of high-quality iron ore.

  • Varieties: Magnetite (best quality, 72% iron), Hematite (most abundant, 60-70% iron), Limonite, Siderite.
  • Distribution: Odisha (Mayurbhanj), Jharkhand (Singhbhum), Chhattisgarh (Bastar), Karnataka (Bellary).
  • Uses: Making pig iron, sponge iron, and steel.

2. Manganese

Manganese is a vital ferro-alloy metal.

  • Uses: Primarily used in the manufacturing of steel to make it hard and rust-resistant. Also used in dry cell batteries, glass, and chemicals.
  • Distribution: Odisha (leading producer), Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh.
ICSE Give Reason: Why is Manganese an essential mineral for the iron and steel industry?
It acts as a scavenger by removing oxygen and sulphur from iron ore during smelting, and it toughens the steel, making it resistant to rusting.

Energy Resources

Conventional (Exhaustible) Sources

  • Coal: Anthracite (best, 90% carbon), Bituminous (most widely used), Lignite (brown coal), Peat. Major coalfields: Jharia (Jharkhand), Raniganj (West Bengal).
  • Petroleum (Mineral Oil): Found in sedimentary rocks. Used for fuel, lubricants, and petrochemicals. Major oilfields: Mumbai High (offshore), Digboi (Assam - oldest), Ankleshwar (Gujarat).
  • Disadvantages: They cause heavy pollution (carbon emissions) and will eventually run out (exhaustible).

Non-Conventional (Renewable) Sources

  • Solar Energy: Abundant in India. Used in solar cookers, water heaters, and photovoltaic cells.
  • Wind Energy: Tamil Nadu and Gujarat lead in wind farms. Clean and renewable.
  • Biogas: Produced by the decomposition of organic waste (cow dung) in the absence of oxygen. Provides both cooking gas and excellent manure.
