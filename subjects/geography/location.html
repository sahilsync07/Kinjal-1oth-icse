Location, Extent & Boundaries

(•‿•)
Loading Geo-Bot...

1. Geographical Limits

  • Latitudinal Extent: Main-land extends from 8°4'N (Kanyakumari) to 37°6'N (Indira Col, Ladakh). The southernmost point of the union is Indira Point (6°45'N) in Great Nicobar.
  • Longitudinal Extent: Extends from 68°7'E (Ghar Mota, Gujarat) to 97°25'E (Kibithu, Arunachal Pradesh). A vast 30° difference, causing a 2-hour time difference between Gujarat and Arunachal.
  • Tropic of Cancer (23°30'N): Divides India into almost two equal parts (Tropical South & Sub-tropical North). Passes through 8 states: Gujarat, Rajasthan, MP, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura, Mizoram.

2. Standard Meridian & IST

  • Standard Meridian: 82°30'E longitude passing through Mirzapur near Prayagraj (UP) is taken as India's Standard Time. It is exactly 5 hours 30 minutes ahead of Greenwich Mean Time (GMT +5:30).

3. Land Frontiers & Neighbors

  • Land Frontier: Approx 15,200 km. Shared with Pakistan and Afghanistan (North-West), China, Nepal, Bhutan (North), Myanmar and Bangladesh (East). Bangladesh shares the longest land border with India.
  • Boundaries: Radcliffe Line (India-Pakistan/Bangladesh), McMahon Line (India-China), Durand Line (India-Afghanistan).

Physiographic Divisions

(•‿•)
Loading Geo-Bot...

1. Northern Mountains (Himalayas)

  • Three parallel ranges:
    • Greater Himalayas (Himadri): Highest, continuous northernmost range. Home to Mt. Everest, Kanchenjunga. Covered with perpetual snow.
    • Lesser Himalayas (Himachal): Rugged middle range. Home to famous hill stations like Shimla, Mussoorie, Darjeeling.
    • Outer Himalayas (Shiwaliks): Southernmost, youngest range. Composed of loose sediments. Valleys between Himachal and Shiwaliks are called Duns (e.g., Dehradun).

2. Great Northern Plains

  • Formed by alluvial deposits of Indus, Ganga, and Brahmaputra rivers.
    • Bhabar: Narrow pebble-ridden belt along foothill slopes where streams disappear underground.
    • Terai: Marshy, swampy, forested belt south of Bhabar where streams reappear.
    • Bhangar: Older, less fertile clayey alluvium forming upland terraces. Contains calcareous kankar deposits.
    • Kadar: Younger, highly fertile sandy silt alluvium replenished by annual river floods.

3. Peninsular Plateau

  • Oldest, highly stable landmass made of ancient crystalline, igneous, and metamorphic rocks. Divides into Malwa Plateau, Deccan Plateau, and Chhota Nagpur Plateau.
  • Western Ghats (Sahyadris): Continuous, higher (avg 900-1600m), can be crossed only via passes (Thal, Bhor, Pal Ghats). Source of major east-flowing rivers.
  • Eastern Ghats: Discontinuous, lower (avg 600m), heavily dissected by east-flowing rivers draining into the Bay of Bengal.

4. Islands

  • Andaman & Nicobar: Volcanic origin (active volcano at Barren Island), larger, elevated.
  • Lakshadweep Islands: Coral origin (coral polyps/atolls), smaller, flat, located in Arabian Sea.

ICSE Give-Reason Masterclass

Scenario / QuestionGeographical Reason
Why does the sun rise 2 hours earlier in Arunachal compared to Gujarat?India spans 30° longitude. Earth rotates 1° in 4 minutes, causing a time difference of 30 × 4 = 120 minutes (2 hours).
Why was 82°30'E selected as the Standard Meridian of India?It lies exactly in the middle of India's longitudinal span (68°E to 97°E) and is a multiple of 7°30' (taken to align with standard global time zones).
Why are the rivers of Peninsular India not navigable year-round?They are entirely rain-fed (seasonal), flowing over rocky terrain with rapids and waterfalls, drying up severely during summer.
Why are Western Ghats continuous while Eastern Ghats are discontinuous?Eastern Ghats have been heavily eroded and cut through (dissected) by major perennial, east-flowing rivers like Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, and Cauvery.
Why are the plains of Northern India highly populated?They consist of deep, fertile alluvial soils, abundant water supply from perennial Himalayan rivers, flat terrain for transport, and a highly favorable agricultural climate.
