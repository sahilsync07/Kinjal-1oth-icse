Alluvial and Black Soils

(•‿•)
Loading Geo-Bot...
Feature Alluvial Soil Black Soil (Regur)
Formation Transported soil (ex-situ). Deposited by rivers (Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra). Residual soil (in-situ). Formed by weathering of volcanic (basalt) rocks.
Characteristics Very fertile. Rich in potash & lime. Deficient in nitrogen & phosphorus. Available as Khadar (new) and Bhangar (old). High moisture retentive capacity. Becomes sticky when wet, develops deep cracks when dry (self-ploughing).
Regions Northern plains, coastal plains, deltas of Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna. Deccan Plateau (Maharashtra, Gujarat, MP).
Crops Wheat, rice, sugarcane, jute. Cotton, sugarcane, wheat, oilseeds.
BOARD TIP "Why is Black Soil called self-ploughing?" — Because it develops deep cracks during summer which helps in soil aeration.

Red and Laterite Soils

(•‿•)
Loading Geo-Bot...
Feature Red Soil Laterite Soil
Formation Residual soil. Weathering of ancient crystalline/metamorphic rocks. Residual soil. Formed by intense leaching due to heavy rain & high temp.
Characteristics Red colour due to high iron oxide content. Porous, friable, non-retentive of moisture. Needs fertilizers. Acidic, poor in organic matter. Becomes hard like brick when dried (used for building).
Regions Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, parts of MP & Odisha. Summits of Western Ghats, Meghalaya, parts of Kerala.
Crops Groundnut, millets, pulses, tobacco (with irrigation). Tea, coffee, rubber, cashew nuts.
KEY TERM Leaching: The process where heavy rainfall washes away the soluble nutrients from the topsoil, leaving behind iron and aluminum oxides.

Soil Erosion & Conservation

(•‿•)
Loading Geo-Bot...

Soil Erosion is the removal of the fertile topsoil by agents like wind and water.

  • Sheet Erosion: Uniform removal of soil in thin layers by surface runoff.
  • Gully Erosion: Runoff water forms deep channels (gullies). Badland topography in Chambal valley.
  • Wind Erosion: Common in arid regions (Rajasthan).

Soil Conservation Methods

Protecting soil from erosion and maintaining its fertility:

  • Afforestation: Planting trees. Roots bind the soil.
  • Terrace Farming: Cutting steps on hill slopes to reduce water flow speed.
  • Contour Ploughing: Ploughing parallel to the contours of a hill slope.
  • Shelter Belts: Planting rows of trees to check wind speed in deserts.
  • Crop Rotation: Growing different crops sequentially to maintain nutrients.
