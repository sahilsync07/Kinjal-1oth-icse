Factors Affecting India's Climate

(•‿•)
Loading Geo-Bot...

India's climate is described as Tropical Monsoon type. Several geographic factors shape it:

Factor Impact on Indian Climate
Latitude The Tropic of Cancer (23½°N) divides India into the tropical zone (south — hotter) and the sub-tropical zone (north — cooler).
Himalayas Act as a climatic divide: block cold Central Asian winds in winter and force moisture-laden monsoon winds to shed rain over India.
Altitude Temperature decreases with altitude (~6.5°C per 1000m). Hill stations like Shimla and Ooty are cooler than plains.
Distance from Sea Coastal areas → equable/maritime climate (small temperature range). Inland areas (e.g., Delhi) → extreme/continental climate (large range).
Western Ghats Cause orographic rainfall on the windward side (Malabar Coast). The leeward side (Deccan Plateau) lies in a rain shadow.
Jet Streams High-altitude winds that influence monsoon onset and withdrawal. The Sub-Tropical Westerly Jet moves north in summer, allowing monsoons to enter.
BOARD TIP "Why are the Himalayas called a climatic divide?" — They prevent cold Siberian winds from entering India and trap monsoon winds, causing them to precipitate.

The Four Seasons of India

(•‿•)
Loading Geo-Bot...

The Indian Meteorological Department recognizes four distinct seasons:

Season Months Key Features
Hot Dry Season (Summer) March – May
  • Intense heating of the landmass; temperatures exceed 45°C in NW India.
  • Loo: Hot, dry, dusty winds over northern plains.
  • Mango Showers: Pre-monsoon rain in Kerala/Karnataka.
  • Kal Baisakhi: Thunderstorms in Bengal/Assam; useful for tea and jute.
  • Low pressure trough forms over northern India → attracts monsoon.
South-West Monsoon (Rainy) June – Sept
  • Moisture-laden winds from Indian Ocean enter India.
  • Arabian Sea Branch: Strikes Malabar Coast first; heavy rain on Western Ghats.
  • Bay of Bengal Branch: Enters NE India, deflected by Himalayas westward across Gangetic Plain.
  • Provides 75% of India's total annual rainfall.
  • "Burst of monsoon": Sudden onset with heavy rain.
Retreating Monsoon (Transition) Oct – Nov
  • Monsoon withdraws from NW India first, moves SE.
  • High heat + humidity = "October Heat".
  • NE Monsoon: Brings rain to Coromandel Coast (Tamil Nadu) from Bay of Bengal.
  • Tropical cyclones may form in Bay of Bengal.
Cold Season (Winter) Dec – Feb
  • Clear skies, low temperatures, pleasant weather across most of India.
  • Western Disturbances: Originate from Mediterranean; bring winter rain to Punjab, Haryana — crucial for Rabi crops (wheat).
  • NE trade winds blow from land to sea → dry season for most of India.

The Monsoon Mechanism

(•‿•)
Loading Geo-Bot...

The word monsoon is derived from the Arabic word "mausim" meaning season. It refers to the seasonal reversal of winds.

Why Do Monsoons Occur?

Factor Explanation
Differential Heating In summer, land heats faster than sea → low pressure over land, high pressure over sea. Winds blow from sea to land (SW Monsoon).
ITCZ Shift The Inter-Tropical Convergence Zone shifts northward over the Gangetic Plain in summer, attracting SE trade winds which cross the equator and become SW monsoon winds.
Jet Streams The Sub-Tropical Westerly Jet moves north of the Himalayas in summer, allowing the Tropical Easterly Jet to bring monsoons. In winter, it returns south.
Coriolis Effect Earth's rotation deflects the SE trade winds to become SW winds after crossing the equator.

Two Branches of SW Monsoon

Branch Path & Effect
Arabian Sea Branch Strikes Malabar Coast first (around 1st June). Heavy orographic rain on windward Western Ghats. Leeward side = rain shadow (e.g., Pune gets less rain than Mumbai).
Bay of Bengal Branch Enters NE India → heavy rain in Meghalaya (Mawsynram = wettest place). Deflected westward by Himalayas → brings rain across the Gangetic Plain.
BOARD FAVOURITE "Why does Mawsynram receive the highest rainfall?" — The funnel-shaped topography of the Khasi Hills traps the Bay of Bengal branch, forcing intense orographic rainfall.
