Topographical maps (topo sheets) are large-scale maps representing both natural and man-made features of an area. In the ICSE Board Exam, Map Study carries a compulsory 10-mark weightage in Part I.

ICSE 2027 SYLLABUS This chapter covers: 4-figure and 6-figure grid references, Scale and distance calculation, Conventional colors and symbols, Contour interpretation (slopes, cliffs, cols), Drainage patterns, Settlement types, and relative heights (e.g., 3r, 6r).

Grid References & Scales

(•‿•)
Loading Geo-Bot...

1. Grid System (Eastings & Northings)

  • The red grid lines on topo sheets are called coordinates.
  • Eastings: Red vertical lines whose numbers increase from West to East. Always read first!
  • Northings: Red horizontal lines whose numbers increase from South to North. Read second!
  • 4-Figure Grid Reference: Locates a 1 km × 1 km grid square. Read the Easting line to the left of the square, then the Northing line below the square (e.g., 2345).
  • 6-Figure Grid Reference: Locates a specific point within a square by dividing the grid square into tenths. The format is Easting-tenth-Northing-tenth (e.g., 234458).

2. Map Scales & Distance Calculation

  • Representative Fraction (R.F.): Standard scale used is 1 : 50,000. This means 1 unit on the map equals 50,000 units on the actual ground.
  • Statement Scale: 2 cm to 1 km (since 50,000 cm = 500 m, 100,000 cm = 1 km = 2 cm on map).
  • Distance Calculation: To find the ground distance between two points, measure the map distance in cm using a ruler or thread, divide by 2, and the result is in kilometers.

2. Contour lines & Slopes

  • Contour lines are brown lines joining places having the same height above sea level. Standard contour interval is 20 meters.
  • Closeness of Contours: Closely spaced contours = Steep Slope; widely spaced contours = Gentle Slope.
  • Special Forms:
    • Ridge: Elongated upland hill with steep slopes.
    • Col: A depression or gap between two mountain peaks.
    • Pass: A navigable, low-level mountain path through a ridge.

3. Drainage & Settlement Patterns

  • Drainage Patterns:
    • Dendritic: Tree-branch like pattern (common in flat plains).
    • Trellis: Streams join main river at right angles (common in folded sedimentary rocks).
    • Radial: Streams flow outward from a central volcanic cone or hill dome.
    • Disappearing Streams: Represented by broken black lines, indicating streams sinking into sand or porous limestone.
  • Settlement Patterns: **Nucleated** (tight cluster of red squares), **Linear** (squares along a road/river bank), and **Dispersed** (widely scattered squares).

ICSE Give-Reason Masterclass

Scenario / QuestionGeographical Reason
What does "6r" written next to a blue well represent?It represents a **relative height** of 6 meters. The lining of the well is 6 meters deep from the ground level to the water surface.
What does "3r" written next to a dry sand stream represent?It represents a **relative height** of 3 meters. The sandy bank of the stream channel has a relative height of 3 meters.
Why do streams in a particular area disappear into broken black lines?The ground is highly porous, composed of dry sand, gravel, or fractured limestone, causing the stream water to seep deep underground.
What does a large yellow patch on a topo sheet signify?It represents agricultural land, indicating that the main occupation of the local population is farming/agriculture.
What does a triangulated station (.340) represent?It represents a point whose exact height above sea level (340 meters) has been determined precisely using geometric triangulation.
