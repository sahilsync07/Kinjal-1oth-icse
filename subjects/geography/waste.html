Types & Sources of Waste

(•‿•)
Loading Geo-Bot...

1. Classification of Waste

  • Biodegradable Waste: Organic wastes that can be broken down into simpler, harmless substances by micro-organisms (bacteria, fungi) over time. Examples: kitchen waste, paper, wood, agricultural residues.
  • Non-biodegradable Waste: Inorganic substances that cannot be broken down by natural decomposers. They accumulate in the environment, causing severe long-term pollution. Examples: plastics, glass, metals, DDT, e-waste.

2. Major Sources of Waste

  • Domestic Waste: Household garbage (food scraps, plastic wraps, old paper, sweepings, sewage).
  • Industrial Waste: Highly toxic chemical effluents, fly ash (from thermal plants), metal scrap, and mineral slag.
  • Agricultural Waste: Crop residues (straw, husks), animal manure, chemical fertilizer runoffs, and pesticide remnants.
  • Municipal Waste: Commercial refuse, street sweepings, construction debris, and sewage sludge generated in urban centers.

3. Need for Waste Management

  1. Public Health: Prevents epidemics (cholera, plague, malaria) spread by vectors breeding in open garbage dumps.
  2. Ecological Balance: Protects aquatic ecosystems from eutrophication (caused by organic waste/fertilizer runoff) and prevents terrestrial soil pollution.
  3. Conservation: Recycling recovers resources, reducing the extraction of virgin materials.

Safe Disposal Methods & 3 Rs

(•‿•)
Loading Geo-Bot...

1. Waste Segregation

The primary step where wet organic waste is separated from dry, recyclable waste (plastics, glass, metals) at the source of generation.

2. Core Disposal Methods

  • Sanitary Landfills: Deep pits where waste is dumped, compacted, and covered with layers of soil daily. Designed with liners to prevent groundwater contamination from toxic fluids (leachate).
  • Composting: Aerobic decomposition of organic waste to produce nutrient-rich humus (compost). **Vermicomposting** uses earthworms (like red wigglers) to accelerate this decomposition.
  • Incineration: Burning waste at extremely high temperatures (900-1000°C) to reduce its volume into ashes. Widely used for hazardous medical waste, but releases harmful gases.
  • Biogas: Anaerobic digestion of animal dung and kitchen waste to produce methane gas (clean fuel) and high-quality slurry manure.

3. The 3 Rs

  • Reduce: Minimizing waste generation (e.g., using cloth bags instead of plastic, buying in bulk).
  • Reuse: Using items multiple times rather than throwing them away (e.g., glass jars for storage).
  • Recycle: Processing waste materials into new products (e.g., melting plastic bottles, recycling old newspaper).

ICSE Give-Reason Masterclass

Scenario / QuestionGeographical Reason
Why is segregation of waste necessary before disposal?It prevents toxic/dry recyclable materials from getting mixed with wet organic waste, which would ruin composting and increase landfill loads.
Why is open dumping of waste highly hazardous?Open piles attract disease-carrying vectors (flies, rats), release foul odors, catch fire easily, and rainwater washes toxic chemicals into groundwater.
Why is vermicomposting preferred over ordinary composting?The digestive track of earthworms enriches the compost with vital microbes and nutrients, and they accelerate the decomposition rate significantly.
Why are sanitary landfills lined with plastic or clay sheets?To prevent highly toxic liquid residue (leachate) from seeping deep into the earth and contaminating vital groundwater aquifers.
Why is incineration highly recommended for hospital waste?Medical waste (syringes, dressings) contains highly infectious pathogens. High-temperature burning completely sterilizes and destroys these biohazards.
