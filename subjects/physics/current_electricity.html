BOARD CRITICAL *Battery EMF vs Terminal Voltage:* Electromotive Force ($\mathcal{E}$) is the potential difference when the cell is in an **open circuit** (no current drawn). Terminal voltage ($V$) is the potential difference when current ($I$) is drawn in a **closed circuit**. The relation is:

1. Current, Voltage & Ohm's Law

  • Current ($I$): The rate of flow of charge: $I = Q/t$. Unit: **Ampere (A)**.
  • Potential Difference ($V$): Work done per unit charge in moving it from one point to another: $V = W/Q$. Unit: **Volt (V)**.
  • Ohm's Law: Current flowing through a metallic conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference across its ends, provided temperature remains constant: $V \propto I \rightarrow V = IR$ (where $R$ is resistance).

2. Resistance & Specific Resistivity

Resistance is the obstruction offered to the flow of current. $R = \rho \times (l / A)$ where $\rho$ is the specific resistance (resistivity), $l$ is length, and $A$ is area of cross-section.

  • $R$ increases with length and temperature; decreases with area.
  • $\rho$ is a **characteristic of the material**; it changes only with material and temperature, **never** with dimensions ($l$ or $A$). Unit of $\rho$: **Ohm-meter ($\Omega$ m)**.

3. Resistor Networks

  • Series Combination: Same current passes through each resistor. Total resistance:
    Rₛ = R₁ + R₂ + R₃ + ...
  • Parallel Combination: Same voltage is applied across each resistor. Total resistance:
    1/Rₚ = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃ + ...

1. Cell EMF, Terminal Voltage & Internal Resistance

A cell has a chemical resistance to ion flow within itself, called **internal resistance ($r$)**.

  • When no current is drawn (Open circuit), Voltmeter reading = **EMF ($\mathcal{E}$)**.
  • When current $I$ is drawn (Closed circuit), Voltmeter reading = **Terminal Voltage ($V$)**.
  • The potential drop inside the cell due to internal resistance is $v = I \times r$.
    Therefore: $\mathcal{E} = V + v = V + Ir \rightarrow V = \mathcal{E} - Ir$.

2. Electrical Energy & Power

  • Electric Power ($P$): Rate at which electrical energy is consumed:
    P = V × I = I²R = V² / R Unit: **Watt (W)**.
  • Electrical Energy ($W$): $W = P \times t$. Commercial unit is **kilowatt-hour (kWh)**.
    1 kWh = 1 kW × 1 hour = 1000 W × 3600 s = 3.6 × 10⁶ Joules
  • Calculating Cost of Electricity:
    Number of units (kWh) = Total Power (in kW) × Total Time (in hours)
