1. Refraction & Cause

Refraction is the change in the direction of path of light when it passes from one transparent medium to another. The primary cause of refraction is the **difference in the speed of light in different media** (Speed in vacuum: $3 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}$, Speed in glass: $2 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}$).

2. Laws of Refraction & Snell's Law

  1. The incident ray, the refracted ray, and the normal at the point of incidence all lie in the same plane.
  2. Snell's Law: The ratio of the sine of the angle of incidence to the sine of the angle of refraction is constant for a given pair of media.
    Refractive Index ($_1\mu_2$) = sin(i) / sin(r) = v₁ / v₂ = λ₁ / λ₂
    Where $v$ is speed and $\lambda$ is wavelength.

3. Real and Apparent Depth

Due to refraction, an object placed in a denser medium (like a coin in water) appears raised when viewed from a rarer medium.
Refractive Index (μ) = Real Depth / Apparent Depth
Shift = Real Depth - Apparent Depth = Real Depth × (1 - 1/μ)

Critical Angle & Total Internal Reflection

1. Critical Angle ($C$)

The angle of incidence in the **denser medium** for which the angle of refraction in the **rarer medium** is $90^\circ$.
sin(C) = 1 / μ (where $\mu$ is refractive index of denser medium relative to rarer).
Factors affecting C: Critical angle increases with temperature and wavelength (Red has larger C than Violet).

2. Total Internal Reflection (TIR)

When a ray of light travelling in a denser medium strikes the interface of a rarer medium at an angle of incidence **greater than the critical angle**, the ray is completely reflected back into the denser medium.
Two Conditions for TIR:

  1. Light must travel from a **denser to a rarer** medium.
  2. Angle of incidence must be **greater than the critical angle** ($i > C$).

3. TIR in Prisms

  • 45°-45°-90° Prism (Right-angled Isosceles): Glass critical angle is ~42°. Light entering normally strikes the inside hypotenuse at 45° ($i > C$), undergoing TIR. Used to deviate rays by 90° (periscopes) or 180° (binoculars).
  • Equilateral Prism (60°-60°-60°): Light entering normally strikes inside face at 60° undergoing TIR, deviating rays by 60°.
