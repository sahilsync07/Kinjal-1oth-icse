Dispersion of Light & Prism

1. Dispersion of White Light

Dispersion is the splitting of white light into its seven constituent colours (VIBGYOR) when it passes through a transparent medium like a glass prism. This happens because different colours have different wavelengths and hence different refractive indices in glass.

  • Violet has the shortest wavelength → highest refractive index → bends the most.
  • Red has the longest wavelength → lowest refractive index → bends the least.
  • Order (VIBGYOR): Violet, Indigo, Blue, Green, Yellow, Orange, Red.

2. The Prism

  • A glass prism has two triangular faces and three rectangular lateral surfaces.
  • The angle between the two refracting surfaces is called the angle of the prism (A).
  • The angle between the incident ray and the emergent ray is called the angle of deviation (δ).
  • Deviation is minimum when the ray passes symmetrically through the prism (i = e).

3. Pure vs Impure Spectrum

Pure SpectrumImpure Spectrum
Colours are distinct and do not overlap.Colours overlap and merge at boundaries.
Obtained using a narrow slit and a convex lens to make rays parallel before entering the prism.Obtained using a wide slit without a lens (divergent rays).

4. Recombination of Colours

Newton proved that the seven colours of the spectrum are not produced by the prism but are already present in white light. By passing the dispersed spectrum through a second inverted prism, the colours recombine to form white light.

1. The Electromagnetic Spectrum

All EM waves travel at the speed of light (3 × 10⁸ m/s) in vacuum. They differ in wavelength and frequency.

Increasing wavelength →
Gamma Rays → X-Rays → UV → Visible Light → IR → Microwaves → Radio Waves
← Increasing frequency

2. Scattering of Light

  • Rayleigh Scattering: Intensity of scattered light ∝ 1/λ⁴. Shorter wavelengths (blue/violet) scatter much more than longer wavelengths (red).
  • Blue sky: Blue light (short λ) is scattered most by air molecules, reaching our eyes from all directions.
  • Red sunsets: At sunset, sunlight travels through a much thicker layer of atmosphere. Blue light gets scattered away completely; only red/orange (long λ) reaches the observer directly.
  • Danger signals are red: Red light has the longest wavelength in visible spectrum, gets scattered the least, and hence travels the farthest through fog/dust.

PropertyDetails
PositionBeyond the violet end of the visible spectrum
