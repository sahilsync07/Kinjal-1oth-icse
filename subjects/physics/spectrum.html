1. Dispersion of Light

The phenomenon of splitting of white light by a prism into its constituent colors is called **dispersion**. The band of colors obtained on a screen is called a **spectrum** (VIBGYOR).

  • Cause of Dispersion: White light contains different colors. In a vacuum, all colors travel at the same speed. In a medium like glass, **different colors travel at different speeds**.
    Since $\mu = c/v$, glass has a different refractive index for each color.
    Violet travels slowest in glass, experiences the highest $\mu$, and deviates the **most**.
    Red travels fastest in glass, experiences the lowest $\mu$, and deviates the **least**.

2. Recombination of Spectrum

Sir Isaac Newton proved that the splitting is purely optical and no new colors are created by the prism. He placed an **inverted second glass prism** behind the first prism. The inverted prism converged the seven colors back into a single beam of white light.

1. Electromagnetic (EM) Spectrum

The complete range of electromagnetic waves arranged in order of increasing wavelength (decreasing frequency):
Gamma < X-Ray < UV < Visible < Infrared < Microwave < Radio

  • Ultraviolet (UV): Wavelengths $10\text{ nm} - 400\text{ nm}$. Sources: electric arc, mercury lamp. Detectors: photographic plate, quartz bulb (since glass absorbs UV). Uses: sterilizing medical tools, detecting fake bank notes.
  • Infrared (IR): Wavelengths $800\text{ nm} - 1\text{ mm}$. Sources: hot glowing bodies. Detectors: thermopile, bolometer, heating effects. Uses: remote controls, photography in fog/night, therapeutic heating. *Rule:* IR has high heating power.

2. Scattering of Light

When light encounters microscopic dust or gas molecules in the atmosphere, it gets absorbed and re-emitted in all directions. This is called **scattering**.

  • Why the sky is blue: Blue light has a short wavelength. It is scattered highly ($I \propto 1/\lambda^4$) by air molecules, coloring the atmosphere blue.
  • Why the sun appears red at sunrise/sunset: At horizon, light travels a longer atmospheric path. Short-wavelength blue/violet lights are completely scattered away before reaching our eyes, leaving behind mostly long-wavelength red/orange light.
  • Space sky: In space, there is no atmosphere to scatter light, so the sky appears completely **black**.
