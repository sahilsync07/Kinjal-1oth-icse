1. Convex vs. Concave Lenses

  • Convex (Converging): Thicker at the middle, thinner at the edges. Converges parallel rays to a single real point (Focus).
  • Concave (Diverging): Thicker at the edges, thinner at the middle. Diverges parallel rays so they appear to originate from a virtual point (Focus).

2. Principal Focus Points

  • First Principal Focus ($F_1$): Point on principal axis such that rays starting from it (convex) or appearing to meet at it (concave) emerge parallel after refraction.
  • Second Principal Focus ($F_2$): Point on principal axis where parallel rays actually meet (convex) or appear to diverge from (concave) after refraction. *Rule:* This is the **primary focus** used in calculations.

4. Concave Lens Rule

For **any** position of the object, a concave lens **always** forms a **virtual, erect, and diminished** image, located between the focus ($F_1$) and the optical center on the same side as the object.

1. Lens Formula

The relation between focal length ($f$), object distance ($u$), and image distance ($v$):

1 / v - 1 / u = 1 / f

2. Magnification ($m$)

The ratio of the height of the image ($I$) to the height of the object ($O$). It is also equal to the ratio of image distance to object distance:

m = height of image (I) / height of object (O) = v / u

  • If $m$ is **positive**, image is **virtual and erect**.
  • If $m$ is **negative**, image is **real and inverted**.

3. Power of a Lens ($P$)

Power is the measure of the deviation (converging or diverging capacity) produced by a lens on rays incident on it. It is the reciprocal of the focal length of the lens expressed in **meters**.

P = 1 / f (in meters) = 100 / f (in cm)

  • Unit of Power: **Dioptre (D)**. $1\text{ D} = 1\text{ m}^{-1}$.
  • Convex lenses have **positive power**; Concave lenses have **negative power**.
