1. Types of Lenses

PropertyConvex Lens (Converging)Concave Lens (Diverging)
ShapeThicker at the centre, thinner at the edgesThinner at the centre, thicker at the edges
Action on LightConverges parallel rays to a real focusDiverges parallel rays; appears to come from a virtual focus
Focal LengthPositive (+f)Negative (−f)
Types of ImagesReal & virtual (depending on object position)Always virtual, erect, diminished
UsesMagnifying glass, camera, projector, spectacles for hypermetropiaPeephole, spectacles for myopia

2. Key Terms

  • Optical Centre (O): The central point of the lens. Any ray passing through O goes undeviated.
  • Principal Axis: The line passing through the optical centre, perpendicular to the lens surface.
  • Principal Focus (F): The point on the principal axis where parallel rays converge (convex) or appear to diverge from (concave). Every lens has two foci — F₁ and F₂ — one on each side.
  • Focal Length (f): Distance between the optical centre and the principal focus.
  • 2F (Centre of Curvature): A point at twice the focal length from O. Object at 2F gives a same-size real image.

3. Three Rules for Drawing Ray Diagrams

  1. Rule 1: A ray parallel to the principal axis, after refraction, passes through F₂ (convex) or appears to come from F₁ (concave).
  2. Rule 2: A ray passing through the optical centre O goes straight through undeviated.
  3. Rule 3: A ray passing through the focus F₁ (or directed towards F₁ for concave), after refraction, emerges parallel to the principal axis.

1. Image Formation by a Convex Lens (all 6 positions)

Object PositionImage PositionNature & Size
At infinityAt F₂Real, inverted, highly diminished (point-sized)
Beyond 2F₁Between F₂ and 2F₂Real, inverted, diminished
At 2F₁At 2F₂Real, inverted, same size
Between F₁ and 2F₁Beyond 2F₂Real, inverted, magnified
At F₁At infinityReal, inverted, highly magnified
Between O and F₁Same side as objectVirtual, erect, magnified (magnifying glass!)

2. Image Formation by a Concave Lens

For any position of the object, a concave lens always forms:

  • A virtual image
  • An erect image
  • A diminished image
  • On the same side as the object, between O and F

3. Key Board-Exam Observations

  • A convex lens acts as a magnifying glass only when the object is between O and F (virtual, erect, magnified image).
  • At 2F, the image is same size — this is the only position where size = 1.
  • As the object moves from infinity towards F, the image moves from F towards infinity (and grows larger).
  • A concave lens never forms a real image for a real object.

Sign Convention, Lens Formula, Magnification & Power

(•‿•)
Loading Phys-Bot...

1. New Cartesian Sign Convention

  • All distances are measured from the optical centre (O).
  • Distances along the direction of incident light (left → right) are positive (+).
  • Distances against the direction of incident light (right → left) are negative (−).
  • Heights above the principal axis are positive (+); below are negative (−).
  • Object distance (u) is always negative (object is always on the left).
  • Focal length: Convex = +f, Concave = −f.

2. Lens Formula

1/v − 1/u = 1/f

Where: v = image distance, u = object distance, f = focal length (all with sign).

3. Magnification (m)

m = h'/h = v/u

  • m is positive → image is erect (virtual). m is negative → image is inverted (real).
  • |m| > 1 → magnified. |m| = 1 → same size. |m| < 1 → diminished.

4. Power of a Lens (P)

P = 1/f (in metres)    Unit: Dioptre (D)

  • Convex lens: P is positive. Concave lens: P is negative.
  • 1 D means focal length = 1 metre. 2 D means focal length = 0.5 metre.
  • Combined power of lenses in contact: P = P₁ + P₂
