Magnetic Fields, Forces & DC Motors

1. Magnetic Field of Current Carriers

  • Straight Wire: Concentric circular magnetic lines around the wire. Direction given by the **Right Hand Thumb Rule** (thumb = current, fingers curled = magnetic field $B$).
  • Loop: Field lines are circular near the wire but straight and parallel at the exact center.
  • Solenoid: A cylindrical coil of insulated copper wire. The magnetic field is **uniform, strong, and parallel** inside the solenoid, resembling a bar magnet. Polarity is determined by the **Clock Rule** (clockwise current = South pole, counter-clockwise current = North pole).

2. Force on a Conductor (Fleming's Left Hand Rule)

When a current-carrying wire is placed in a magnetic field, it experiences a mechanical force. The direction of this force is determined by **Fleming's Left Hand Rule**:

  • Forefinger: Points in the direction of the Magnetic Field ($B$).
  • Middle Finger: Points in the direction of the Current ($I$).
  • Thumb: Points in the direction of the resulting Force (Motion, $F$).

3. The DC Motor

A device that converts **electrical energy into mechanical energy** based on the magnetic force acting on a current coil.

  • Commutator (Split Rings): Reverses the direction of current in the armature coil every **half-rotation (180°)**, ensuring that the coil continues to rotate in a single, continuous direction.
  • Brushes: Carbon blocks that maintain electrical contact with the rotating split rings.

1. Electromagnetic Induction (EMI)

When the magnetic flux linked with a circuit changes, an electromotive force (EMF) is induced in it.
Faraday's Laws:

  1. An EMF is induced in a coil only as long as there is a **change in the magnetic flux** linked with the coil.
  2. The magnitude of induced EMF is directly proportional to the **rate of change of magnetic flux** linked with the coil: $e = -N \frac{\Delta \Phi}{\Delta t}$.
Lenz's Law: Direction of induced EMF opposes the flux change (marked by the negative sign).

2. AC Generator

Converts **mechanical energy into electrical energy** using electromagnetic induction.

  • Uses **Slip Rings** (complete rings, not split) which maintain connection to the same side of the coil, delivering alternating current (AC) to the external circuit.

3. Transformers

Devices that alter alternating voltage without changing frequency. They operate strictly on **Mutual Induction** between two coils: Primary ($P$) and Secondary ($S$).

  • Formula:
    Vₛ / Vₚ = Nₛ / Nₚ = Iₚ / Iₛ
  • Step-Up Transformer: Increases voltage ($V_s > V_p$ because $N_s > N_p$). Current decreases ($I_s < I_p$). Secondary wire is thinner.
  • Step-Down Transformer: Decreases voltage ($V_s < V_p$ because $N_s < N_p$). Current increases ($I_s > I_p$). Secondary wire is thicker.
  • Energy Losses: Copper loss ($I^2R$ heating), Iron loss (Eddy currents in core—reduced by **lamination**), Hysteresis loss, and Magnetic flux leakage.
