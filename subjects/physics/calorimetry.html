1. Heat Capacity ($C'$) vs Specific Heat Capacity ($c$)

  • Heat Capacity ($C'$): Heat energy required to raise the temperature of the *entire body* by $1\text{ K}$ (or $1^\circ\text{C}$):
    C' = Q / ΔT = m × c Unit: **$\text{J K}^{-1}$** or **$\text{J }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$**. It depends on both the material and mass of the body.
  • Specific Heat Capacity ($c$): Heat energy required to raise the temperature of a *unit mass (1 kg or 1 g)* of the substance by $1\text{ K}$ (or $1^\circ\text{C}$):
    c = Q / (m × ΔT) → Q = m × c × ΔT Unit: **$\text{J kg}^{-1}\text{ K}^{-1}$** or **$\text{J g}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$**. It is a **characteristic property** of the material and does not depend on its mass.

2. Principle of Calorimetry (Method of Mixtures)

When a hot body is mixed with a cold body in an insulated container (Calorimeter):

Heat Lost by Hot Body = Heat Gained by Cold Body
m₁ × c₁ × (T₁ - T_mix) = m₂ × c₂ × (T_mix - T₂)

Note: A calorimeter is made of **thin copper** because copper has a low specific heat capacity (requires minimal heat to reach thermal equilibrium) and is highly conductive, ensuring rapid heat transfer.

1. What is Latent Heat ($L$)?

Latent heat (hidden heat) is the heat absorbed or released by a substance during a **change of state** (phase transition) at **constant temperature**.

  • During a change of state, heat is used to break/form intermolecular bonds, **not** to increase the kinetic energy of molecules. Hence, the temperature remains absolutely constant.
  • Specific Latent Heat ($l$): The amount of heat required to change the state of $1\text{ kg}$ (or $1\text{ g}$) of a substance at its transition temperature:
    Q = m × l Unit: **$\text{J kg}^{-1}$** or **$\text{J g}^{-1}$**.

2. Values & Phenomena of Latent Heat

  • Specific Latent Heat of Fusion of Ice ($l_{ice}$): **$336\text{ J g}^{-1}$** (or $3.36 \times 10^5\text{ J kg}^{-1}$).
    Phenomenon: Lakes do not freeze solid instantly in winter, and soft drinks are cooled much more effectively by $0^\circ\text{C}$ ice than by $0^\circ\text{C}$ water because each gram of melting ice absorbs an additional $336\text{ J}$ of heat from the drink!
  • Specific Latent Heat of Vaporization of Steam ($l_{steam}$): **$2260\text{ J g}^{-1}$** (or $2.26 \times 10^6\text{ J kg}^{-1}$).
    Phenomenon: Burns caused by $100^\circ\text{C}$ steam are much more severe than those caused by $100^\circ\text{C}$ boiling water because steam releases an extra $2260\text{ J}$ of heat per gram upon condensing on the skin!

3. The Heating Curve

A plot of temperature vs time for a heated solid:

  • Slanted sections: Temperature rises. Heat is $Q = mc\Delta T$. Kinetic energy increases.
  • Flat sections: Change of state (melting or boiling). Temperature is constant. Heat is $Q = mL$. Potential energy increases.
