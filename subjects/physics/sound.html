Since the persistence of human hearing is **$0.1\text{ seconds}$** and the speed of sound in air is ~**$340\text{ m/s}$**, the minimum distance required is:
d ≥ (340 × 0.1) / 2 = 17 meters

1. What is an Echo?

An **echo** is the sound heard after reflection from a distant obstacle, such as a cliff, hillside, or wall, after the original sound has ceased.

2. Two Essential Conditions for hearing an Echo

  1. Time interval: The reflected sound must reach the ear at least **$0.1\text{ seconds}$** after the original sound, because the sensation of a sound persists in our brain for $0.1$ seconds (persistence of hearing).
  2. Distance: The obstacle must be far enough. In air ($v \approx 340\text{ m/s}$), the minimum distance is **$17\text{ meters}$**. In water ($v \approx 1400\text{ m/s}$), the minimum distance is **$70\text{ meters}$**.

3. Sonar (Sound Navigation and Ranging)

Utilizes **ultrasonic waves** (frequency > 20 kHz) because they are highly directional, travel long distances without deviation, and are not absorbed easily by water.
Sonar Depth Formula: **$d = v \times t / 2$** (since waves travel to the seabed and back).

1. Types of Vibrations

  • Natural (Free) Vibrations: Periodic vibrations of a body in the **absence of any external force or resistive medium** (vacuum). They vibrate with constant amplitude and natural frequency.
  • Damped Vibrations: Periodic vibrations of a body in a **resistive medium** (like air). The amplitude decreases continuously with time due to friction, and vibrations eventually die out.
  • Forced Vibrations: Vibrations produced in a body when it is forced to vibrate under the influence of an **external periodic force** with a frequency different from its natural frequency.

2. Resonance (Acoustic Amplification)

Resonance is a special case of forced vibrations where the frequency of the applied external periodic force is **equal to the natural frequency** of the body. The body vibrates with a **very large amplitude**.

  • Examples: Soldiers breaking step while crossing suspension bridges, tuning a radio, shattering of a wine glass by a singer, and matching tuning forks.
