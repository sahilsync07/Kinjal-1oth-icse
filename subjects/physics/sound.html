Reflection of Sound & Echoes

(•‿•)
Loading Phys-Bot...

1. Reflection of Sound

Sound waves, like light, obey the laws of reflection: (1) Angle of incidence = Angle of reflection. (2) Incident wave, reflected wave, and normal all lie in the same plane. Sound reflects best from large, hard, polished surfaces (walls, cliffs, metal sheets).

2. Echo

An echo is a repetition of sound heard due to reflection from a hard surface. For a distinct echo, the reflected sound must reach the ear at least 0.1 seconds after the original sound.

Minimum distance for echo = v × t / 2 = 340 × 0.1 / 2 = 17 metres

The factor of 2 is because sound travels to the reflecting surface AND back.

3. SONAR (Sound Navigation And Ranging)

SONAR uses ultrasonic waves (frequency > 20,000 Hz) to measure the depth of the sea or detect underwater objects.

Depth (d) = v × t / 2

where v = speed of sound in water (~1500 m/s) and t = time for the echo to return.

4. Applications of Reflection of Sound

  • Stethoscope: Sound of heartbeat reflects through the rubber tube.
  • Megaphone/Loudspeaker: Sound reflects from the cone and is directed towards the audience.
  • Soundboard in auditoriums: Curved reflective surfaces behind the speaker direct sound towards the audience.

Vibrations & Resonance

(•‿•)
Loading Phys-Bot...

1. Natural (Free) Vibrations

When a body vibrates at its own natural frequency without any external periodic force, it undergoes natural vibrations. The natural frequency depends on the body's shape, size, and material. Example: A tuning fork struck once vibrates at its natural frequency.

2. Damped Vibrations

Natural vibrations that gradually decrease in amplitude due to friction/air resistance are damped vibrations. Energy is lost as heat. Eventually, the vibrations stop. Example: A struck tuning fork gradually becomes silent.

3. Forced Vibrations

When a body is made to vibrate at a frequency other than its natural frequency by an external periodic force, the vibrations are forced vibrations. The body vibrates at the frequency of the applied force. Example: Vibrations of the sounding board of a guitar (forced to vibrate at the string's frequency).

4. Resonance

Resonance occurs when the frequency of the external periodic force exactly matches the natural frequency of the body. The amplitude of vibration becomes maximum. This is the most important concept for boards!

  • Examples: Soldiers breaking step on a bridge (to avoid resonance that could collapse it), a child on a swing being pushed at the right time, shattering of glass by a specific musical note, Tacoma Narrows Bridge collapse.
  • Condition: Frequency of applied force = Natural frequency of the body.

5. Reverberation

The persistence of sound in a hall due to repeated reflections from walls, ceiling, and floor is called reverberation. Excessive reverberation makes speech unclear. It is controlled by using sound-absorbing materials (curtains, carpets, acoustic tiles).

Characteristics of Sound

(•‿•)
Loading Phys-Bot...

1. Loudness

  • Depends on the amplitude of vibration of the source.
  • Larger amplitude → louder sound. Also depends on surface area of the vibrating body.
  • Measured in decibels (dB). Normal conversation ≈ 60 dB. Threshold of pain ≈ 120 dB.

2. Pitch

  • Depends on the frequency of vibration.
  • Higher frequency → higher pitch (shriller). Lower frequency → lower pitch (deeper/bass).
  • A woman's voice is shriller (higher pitch) than a man's voice because the frequency is higher.
  • Audible range: 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz. Below 20 Hz = infrasonic. Above 20,000 Hz = ultrasonic.

3. Quality (Timbre)

  • The characteristic that distinguishes two sounds of the same pitch and loudness played by different instruments.
  • Depends on the waveform (number and intensity of overtones/harmonics).
  • A tuning fork produces a pure tone (no overtones). Musical instruments produce complex tones with overtones.

4. Noise vs Music

NoiseMusic
Irregular, jarring vibrations with no patternRegular, periodic, pleasing vibrations
Has no definite pitch or frequencyHas a definite pitch and frequency
Example: traffic honking, drillingExample: sitar, flute, piano
