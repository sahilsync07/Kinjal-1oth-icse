Machine Terminology & Levers

(•‿•)

1. Core Technical Terms

  • Load (L): The resistive or opposing force to be overcome by the machine.
  • Effort (E): The force applied to the machine to overcome the load.
  • Mechanical Advantage (MA): The ratio of the load to the effort.

    MA = Load (L) / Effort (E)

    Note: MA has no unit because it is a ratio of two similar forces. MA > 1 (force multiplier), MA < 1 (speed multiplier), MA = 1 (changes direction of force).
  • Velocity Ratio (VR): The ratio of the velocity of effort to the velocity of load. Alternatively, it is the ratio of the distance moved by effort to the distance moved by load in the same time.

    VR = Distance moved by Effort (dE) / Distance moved by Load (dL)

    Note: VR is constant for a given machine design and does not change due to friction.
  • Efficiency (η): The ratio of the useful work done by the machine (Work Output) to the work done on the machine (Work Input).

    η = Work Output (Wout) / Work Input (Win)

2. Relationship between MA, VR, and Efficiency

Mechanical Advantage (MA) = Efficiency (η) × Velocity Ratio (VR)

In an ideal machine (no friction, weightless moving parts), η = 1 (100%), so MA = VR. In a real machine, η < 100%, so MA < VR.

3. Levers

A lever is a rigid, straight (or bent) bar capable of turning about a fixed point called the fulcrum (F).

Principle of Levers: Load × Load Arm = Effort × Effort Arm. Therefore:

MA = Effort Arm / Load Arm

Pulley Systems

(•‿•)

1. Single Fixed Pulley

  • A pulley whose axis of rotation is fixed in space.
  • Ideal MA = 1, VR = 1, η = 100%.
  • Purpose: It does not multiply force. It is used solely to change the direction of force (effort) to a more convenient downward direction.

2. Single Movable Pulley

  • A pulley whose axis of rotation is movable (supported by two segments of string).
  • Ideal MA = 2, VR = 2, η = 100%.
  • Purpose: It acts as a force multiplier. An effort equal to half the load is theoretically required.

3. Block and Tackle Pulley System

A combination of several pulleys, where one block of pulleys (upper) is fixed, and the other block (lower) is movable and attached to the load. A single string is wound around all pulleys.

  • Velocity Ratio (VR): Always equal to the number of pulleys (n) in the system.

    VR = n

  • Ideal Mechanical Advantage (MA): Equal to n (for 100% efficiency).

    MA = n (Ideal)

  • Real Mechanical Advantage (MA): Less than n because of the weight of the lower movable block (w) and friction.

    MA = n - (w / Effort)

    Or expressing effort needed: Effort (E) = (Load + w) / n (neglecting string friction).
