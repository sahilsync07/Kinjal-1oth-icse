1. Definition of Work

Work is said to be done only when a force applied on a body produces displacement in the body. If the force acts at an angle θ to the displacement, the work done is:

Work (W) = F × s × cos(θ)

2. Conditions for Work Done

  • Positive Work (θ < 90°): Displacement is in the direction of the force. Max work when θ = 0° (W = F s).
  • Zero Work (θ = 90° or s = 0): Displacement is perpendicular to the force. Example: A coolie walking with a load on his head, or a satellite orbiting the Earth.
  • Negative Work (θ > 90°): Displacement is opposite to the force. Example: Work done by friction or brakes (θ = 180°, W = -Fs).

3. Power

Power is defined as the rate of doing work. It represents how fast work is completed.

Power (P) = Work done / Time = F × (s / t) = F × v

1. Mechanical Energy

Mechanical energy is the energy possessed by a body due to its position, configuration, or state of motion. It exists in two main forms: Potential Energy and Kinetic Energy.

2. Monospace Comparison Table

Feature Potential Energy (PE / U) Kinetic Energy (KE / K)
Definition Energy possessed by a body due to its position or deformed shape. Energy possessed by a body due to its state of motion.
Formulas U = mgh (gravitational)
Elastic U (e.g. spring)		 K = ½ m v²
K = p² / 2m (where p = mv)
Examples Water stored in a dam, a compressed spring, a stretched bow. A speeding bullet, running athlete, flowing river, falling stone.

3. Key Derivations & Relations

  • Potential Energy (U = mgh): Work done to lift a mass $m$ to height $h$ is $W = \text{Force} \times \text{displacement} = mg \times h = mgh$. This work is stored as potential energy.
  • Kinetic Energy-Momentum Relation:

    K = ½ m v² = ½ m (p/m)² = p² / (2m)

    This means momentum can be calculated as: p = √(2mK).

Conservation of Energy

(•‿•)
Loading Phys-Bot...

1. Law of Conservation of Energy

According to this law, energy can neither be created nor destroyed; it can only be transformed from one form to another. The total mechanical energy ($E = K + U$) of a system remains constant if there are no dissipative forces like air resistance.

2. Mathematical Proof: Freely Falling Body

Consider a body of mass $m$ falling freely from a height $h$ above the ground:

Position Potential Energy (U) Kinetic Energy (K) Total Energy (E = U + K)
At Top (Height h) mgh 0 mgh
At Midpoint (Height h-x) mg(h - x) mgx (since velocity v² = 2gx) mgh
At Ground level 0 mgh (since velocity v² = 2gh) mgh

Since total energy is $mgh$ at all points, mechanical energy is conserved during free fall.

3. Case of a Swinging Simple Pendulum

  • At extreme positions (left and right), the bob momentarily halts, so speed $v = 0$. Thus, kinetic energy is zero and potential energy is maximum ($U = mgh$).
  • At the mean position, the bob is at its lowest height, so potential energy is minimum (zero) and its kinetic energy is maximum ($K = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 = mgh$).
  • At all other intermediate points, the energy is partly potential and partly kinetic, but the total sum $E = K + U$ is always constant.
